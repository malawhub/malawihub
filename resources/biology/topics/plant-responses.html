🌻 Plant Responses

Tropisms, plant hormones and responses to environmental stimuli.

Learning Objectives

  • Define a stimulus and a response.
  • Explain tropisms in plants.
  • Distinguish between phototropism and gravitropism.
  • Explain the role of auxin in plant growth responses.
  • Relate plant responses to survival.

1. Stimulus and Response

A stimulus is a change in the internal or external environment that can be detected by an organism.

A response is a change in behaviour, growth or activity resulting from a stimulus.

Plants respond to environmental stimuli such as light, gravity, water and chemicals.

2. Tropisms

A tropism is a directional growth response of a plant organ to a directional stimulus.

  • Positive tropism: growth towards the stimulus.
  • Negative tropism: growth away from the stimulus.

3. Phototropism

Phototropism is the growth response of a plant to light.

Young shoots usually show positive phototropism because they grow towards light.

Roots commonly show negative phototropism because they tend to grow away from light.

4. Gravitropism

Gravitropism, also called geotropism, is a growth response to gravity.

  • Roots usually show positive gravitropism because they grow in the direction of gravity.
  • Shoots usually show negative gravitropism because they grow against gravity.

5. Auxin

Auxin is a plant growth hormone involved in controlling cell elongation and growth responses.

Auxin is produced in growing regions such as shoot tips and can move through plant tissues.

6. Auxin and Shoot Phototropism

When light comes mainly from one side of a young shoot, auxin becomes distributed unevenly.

In shoots, a greater concentration of auxin on the shaded side stimulates greater cell elongation there. The shaded side therefore grows faster, causing the shoot to bend towards the light.

7. Auxin and Roots

Roots respond differently to auxin concentration. Relatively high auxin concentrations can inhibit root cell elongation. This difference contributes to the characteristic response of roots to gravity.

8. Importance of Plant Responses

  • Shoots grow towards light, improving light absorption for photosynthesis.
  • Roots grow into the soil, improving access to water and mineral ions.
  • Roots respond to gravity, helping establish a stable position in the soil.
  • Growth responses help plants adjust to changing environmental conditions.

9. Investigating Phototropism

A simple investigation can compare seedlings exposed to light from different directions.

Experimental Design

  • Use seedlings of similar age and size.
  • Provide light from one controlled direction.
  • Use a suitable control group where required.
  • Keep water, temperature and other conditions constant.
  • Measure the direction or angle of growth.
  • Repeat the investigation to improve reliability.

10. Other Plant Responses

Plants can also respond to touch, water availability and chemicals. Some responses involve changes in growth, while others may involve faster movements or changes in cell turgor.

MANEB-Style Practice

  1. Define a stimulus.
  2. Define phototropism.
  3. Distinguish between positive and negative tropism.
  4. State the usual gravitropic response of roots.
  5. Explain why a young shoot bends towards light.
  6. State the role of auxin in shoot phototropism.
  7. Explain why plant responses are important for survival.
  8. Suggest two variables that should be controlled in a phototropism experiment.

Answers

  1. A stimulus is a change in the environment detected by an organism.
  2. Phototropism is a directional growth response to light.
  3. Positive tropism is growth towards the stimulus; negative tropism is growth away from it.
  4. Roots usually show positive gravitropism.
  5. Auxin becomes concentrated on the shaded side, where it promotes greater cell elongation in shoots. The shoot therefore bends towards the light.
  6. Auxin causes differential growth in the shoot, producing bending towards light.
  7. Responses help plants obtain light, water and mineral ions and position themselves in favourable conditions.
  8. Examples include temperature, water supply, seedling age and light intensity.

Common Mistakes

  • Do not confuse a stimulus with a response.
  • Remember that tropism is directional growth.
  • Do not state that roots and shoots always respond identically to auxin.
  • For phototropism, explain unequal growth rather than simply saying the plant "moves" towards light.

Examination Tips

  • Know the meanings of phototropism and gravitropism.
  • Remember: roots are generally positively gravitropic and shoots negatively gravitropic.
  • When explaining auxin questions, use the terms unequal distribution, cell elongation and differential growth.
  • Practise drawing experiments showing the direction of light and plant growth.
