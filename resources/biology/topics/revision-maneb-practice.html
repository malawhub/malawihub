Answers: Section A

  1. Movement, respiration, sensitivity, growth, reproduction, excretion and nutrition.
  2. A cell is the basic structural and functional unit of living organisms.
  3. Chloroplasts and large permanent vacuoles.
  4. It is the main site of aerobic respiration and ATP production.
  5. Diffusion is the net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration.
  6. Osmosis is the net movement of water molecules through a partially permeable membrane from higher water potential to lower water potential.
  7. Xylem transports water and mineral ions from roots to other parts of the plant and also provides support.
  8. Phloem transports organic substances such as sugars around the plant.
  9. Carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen.
  10. Nitrate/nitrogen and magnesium are examples.
  11. Transpiration is the loss of water vapour from plant surfaces, mainly through stomata.
  12. Phototropism is the directional growth response of a plant to light.
  13. Fish, amphibians, reptiles, birds and mammals.
  14. They transport oxygen around the body using haemoglobin.
  15. Right atrium, right ventricle, left atrium and left ventricle.
  16. The kidney removes nitrogenous waste and regulates water and mineral-ion balance.
  17. Excretion is the removal of metabolic waste products from an organism.
  18. Insulin lowers blood glucose concentration by promoting uptake and storage of glucose.
  19. A rapid automatic response to a stimulus that does not require conscious decision-making.
  20. Fertilisation is the fusion of the nuclei of male and female gametes.
  21. A pathogen is an infectious agent that causes disease.
  22. Vaccination, good hygiene, safe water, sanitation, vector control and safer sexual practices are examples.
  23. A gene is a section of DNA containing genetic information.
  24. A mutation is a change in the DNA sequence.
  25. Natural selection is the process by which inherited characteristics that improve survival or reproduction become more common in a population over generations.
  26. A group of organisms of the same species living in the same area at the same time.
  27. They decompose dead organisms and recycle nutrients.
  28. Biotechnology is the application of organisms, cells or biological processes to produce useful products or solve problems.
  29. Blue-black colour.
  30. Eyepiece magnification × objective magnification.

Section B: Structured Questions

Question 1: Cells

A student observes a cell using a microscope.

  1. State the function of the nucleus.
  2. State the function of the cell membrane.
  3. Give two differences between a plant cell and an animal cell.
  4. Explain why a root hair cell is well adapted for absorbing water.

Answer

  1. The nucleus contains genetic material and controls cell activities.
  2. The cell membrane controls movement of substances into and out of the cell.
  3. Plant cells normally have a cell wall and chloroplasts, while animal cells do not.
  4. It has a long extension that increases surface area for absorption and contains a partially permeable cell membrane. The cell has a large vacuole and suitable water-potential conditions for water uptake by osmosis.

Question 2: Photosynthesis

A student investigates the effect of light intensity on photosynthesis.

  1. Identify the independent variable.
  2. Identify the dependent variable.
  3. State two variables that should be controlled.
  4. Explain why the rate of photosynthesis may stop increasing when light intensity becomes very high.

Answer

  1. Light intensity.
  2. The rate of photosynthesis, measured for example by oxygen production.
  3. Temperature and carbon dioxide concentration are examples.
  4. Another factor such as carbon dioxide concentration or temperature may become limiting.

Question 3: Human Transport

Explain how blood travels through the human heart from the vena cava until it leaves through the aorta.

Answer

Blood enters the right atrium through the vena cava. It passes through the tricuspid valve into the right ventricle. The right ventricle pumps it through the pulmonary artery to the lungs. Oxygenated blood returns through the pulmonary vein to the left atrium. It passes through the mitral valve into the left ventricle. The left ventricle pumps blood through the aorta to the rest of the body.

Question 4: Excretion

Explain how the kidney helps maintain the composition of the internal environment.

Answer

The kidneys filter the blood and remove nitrogenous waste such as urea. Useful substances are selectively reabsorbed. The amount of water and mineral ions retained or excreted is regulated, helping maintain the body's internal balance.

Question 5: Reproduction

Describe the sequence of events from ovulation to implantation.

Answer

An ovary releases a mature egg during ovulation. The egg enters the oviduct. If sperm are present, fertilisation may occur in the oviduct. The resulting zygote divides repeatedly by mitosis as it travels towards the uterus. The developing embryo becomes attached to and embedded in the uterine lining during implantation.

Question 6: Immunity

Explain how vaccination protects an individual against an infectious disease.

Answer

A vaccine introduces harmless forms or components of a pathogen's antigens. The immune system responds by producing specific antibodies and memory cells. If the same pathogen later enters the body, memory cells allow a faster and stronger immune response, helping prevent or reduce disease.

Question 7: Genetics

Two heterozygous organisms with genotype Tt are crossed. T is dominant over t.

  1. State the possible gametes produced by each parent.
  2. State the genotype ratio.
  3. State the phenotype ratio.
  4. What is the probability of an offspring having genotype tt?

Answer

  1. T and t.
  2. 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt.
  3. 3 dominant : 1 recessive.
  4. 25% or 1/4.

Question 8: Ecology

Explain how energy is transferred through a food chain and why less energy is available at higher trophic levels.

Answer

Producers capture light energy and store it as chemical energy. When organisms feed, some of this energy is transferred to the next trophic level. Energy is lost through respiration, heat, movement, waste and other processes. Therefore, less energy is generally available at higher trophic levels.

Section C: Practical Biology

Question 1

A student places equal-sized potato pieces into solutions of different concentrations.

  1. What process is being investigated?
  2. What should be measured?
  3. State two controlled variables.
  4. Explain why the potato pieces may gain or lose mass.

Answer

  1. Osmosis.
  2. Change in mass or length of the potato pieces.
  3. Temperature, potato-piece size, volume of solution and duration.
  4. Water moves across the partially permeable cell membranes by osmosis according to the difference in water potential between the potato cells and surrounding solution.

Question 2

A student obtains three readings of 12 cm, 14 cm and 13 cm.

Calculate the mean.

Answer

Mean = (12 + 14 + 13) ÷ 3 = 13 cm.

Section D: Long-Answer Practice

Question 1

Describe the structure of a human kidney and explain how urine is formed.

Key Points for a Full Answer

  • Kidney contains many nephrons.
  • Blood enters through the renal artery.
  • Ultrafiltration occurs in the renal corpuscle.
  • Small molecules enter the filtrate.
  • Useful substances are selectively reabsorbed.
  • Water reabsorption is regulated according to body requirements.
  • Urea and excess substances remain in the filtrate.
  • Urine passes through collecting ducts and the ureter to the bladder.

Question 2

Explain how a green plant is adapted for photosynthesis.

Key Points for a Full Answer

  • Leaves provide a large surface area.
  • Leaves are thin, giving a short diffusion distance.
  • Chloroplasts contain chlorophyll.
  • Chloroplasts are abundant in palisade cells.
  • Stomata allow carbon dioxide to enter.
  • Veins supply water and mineral ions.
  • Xylem transports water to the leaf.
  • Phloem transports products of photosynthesis away from the leaf.

Question 3

Explain natural selection using antibiotic resistance as an example.

Key Points for a Full Answer

  • Bacteria show genetic variation.
  • Some bacteria possess resistance alleles.
  • Antibiotic treatment kills susceptible bacteria.
  • Resistant bacteria survive.
  • Survivors reproduce.
  • Resistance alleles are passed to offspring.
  • The proportion of resistant bacteria increases over generations.

Important Definitions

Term Meaning
Diffusion Net movement of particles from higher to lower concentration.
Osmosis Movement of water through a partially permeable membrane from higher to lower water potential.
Active transport Movement of substances against a concentration gradient using energy.
Respiration Controlled release of energy from organic molecules in cells.
Excretion Removal of metabolic waste products.
Homeostasis Maintenance of relatively stable internal conditions.
Hormone A chemical messenger transported in the body to affect target cells.
Pathogen An infectious agent that causes disease.
Antibody A specific protein produced by lymphocytes that binds to an antigen.
Gene A section of DNA containing genetic information.
Allele An alternative form of a gene.
Mutation A change in DNA sequence.
Population Organisms of the same species living in the same area at the same time.
Ecosystem A community of organisms and their non-living environment.
Biotechnology Use of biological organisms or processes to produce useful products or solve problems.
