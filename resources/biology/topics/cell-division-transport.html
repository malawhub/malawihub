🧬 Cell Division & Transport

Mitosis, meiosis, diffusion, osmosis and active transport.

Learning Objectives

  • Explain why cells divide.
  • Describe the main stages and importance of mitosis.
  • Describe the significance of meiosis.
  • Explain diffusion, osmosis and active transport.
  • Apply transport principles to biological examples.

1. Why Cells Divide

Cell division is important for growth, replacement of damaged cells, repair of tissues and reproduction. In multicellular organisms, new cells are produced from existing cells.

2. Mitosis

Mitosis produces two genetically similar daughter cells. The chromosome number remains the same as in the parent cell.

Importance of Mitosis

  • Growth of multicellular organisms.
  • Replacement of worn-out cells.
  • Repair of damaged tissues.
  • Asexual reproduction in some organisms.

Main Stages

  1. Prophase: chromosomes condense and become visible; the nuclear envelope breaks down.
  2. Metaphase: chromosomes arrange themselves at the equator of the cell.
  3. Anaphase: sister chromatids separate and move to opposite poles.
  4. Telophase: new nuclear envelopes form around the chromosomes.
  5. Cytokinesis: the cytoplasm divides, producing two daughter cells.

3. Meiosis

Meiosis is a specialised form of cell division that produces haploid gametes. It involves two successive divisions and results in genetically different cells.

Importance of Meiosis

  • Produces gametes for sexual reproduction.
  • Halves the chromosome number.
  • Creates genetic variation among offspring.

4. Mitosis Compared with Meiosis

Feature Mitosis Meiosis
Number of divisions One Two
Number of daughter cells Two Usually four
Chromosome number Maintained Halved
Genetic similarity Usually genetically similar Genetically different
Main role Growth and repair Gamete production

5. Diffusion

Diffusion is the net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration, down a concentration gradient.

Diffusion does not require cellular energy from ATP.

Factors Affecting Diffusion

  • Concentration gradient.
  • Temperature.
  • Surface area.
  • Diffusion distance.
  • Size of the particles.

6. Osmosis

Osmosis is the net movement of water molecules through a partially permeable membrane from a region of higher water potential to a region of lower water potential.

Plant Cells in Different Solutions

  • In a dilute solution, water enters the cell and the cell becomes turgid.
  • In a concentrated solution, water leaves the cell.
  • When a plant cell loses enough water, the cell membrane pulls away from the cell wall. This is called plasmolysis.

7. Active Transport

Active transport is the movement of substances across a membrane from a lower concentration to a higher concentration using energy released by respiration.

Examples

  • Mineral ion uptake by root hair cells.
  • Absorption of some nutrients from the small intestine.

8. Comparing Transport Processes

Process Substance Direction Energy Required
Diffusion Particles such as gases High to low concentration No
Osmosis Water High to low water potential No
Active transport Solutes/ions Low to high concentration Yes

9. Practical Investigation: Osmosis

Potato cylinders can be placed in solutions of different concentrations and their changes in mass measured.

  • Measure the initial mass.
  • Place equal-sized pieces into different solutions.
  • Keep temperature and time constant.
  • Remove the pieces and blot them carefully.
  • Measure the final mass.
  • Calculate percentage change in mass.

Percentage change = (change in mass ÷ original mass) × 100

MANEB-Style Practice

  1. State two functions of mitosis.
  2. Explain why meiosis is important in sexual reproduction.
  3. Define diffusion.
  4. Define osmosis.
  5. Explain why active transport requires energy.
  6. Explain what happens to a plant cell placed in a very concentrated solution.
  7. A potato cylinder has an initial mass of 5.0 g and a final mass of 5.5 g. Calculate the percentage change in mass.

Answers

  1. Growth, repair and replacement of damaged or worn-out cells.
  2. It produces haploid gametes and creates genetic variation. Fusion of two gametes restores the diploid chromosome number.
  3. Net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to lower concentration.
  4. Net movement of water molecules through a partially permeable membrane from higher water potential to lower water potential.
  5. It moves substances against their concentration gradient and therefore requires energy from respiration.
  6. Water leaves the cell by osmosis. The vacuole shrinks and the membrane may pull away from the cell wall, causing plasmolysis.
  7. Change = 5.5 − 5.0 = 0.5 g. Percentage change = (0.5 ÷ 5.0) × 100 = 10%.

Common Mistakes

  • Do not define osmosis as movement of any particles; it specifically involves water.
  • Do not say diffusion requires energy from respiration.
  • Active transport moves substances against a concentration gradient.
  • Do not confuse chromosome number with the amount of DNA present.

Examination Tips

  • Learn the differences between mitosis and meiosis.
  • Use the terms concentration gradient, partially permeable membrane and water potential correctly.
  • Show calculations clearly in practical questions.
  • When explaining an experiment, identify variables that must be controlled.
