🌱 Living Things & Environment

Biology notes on characteristics of living organisms, habitats, ecosystems and environmental sustainability.

Learning Objectives

  • Describe the characteristics of living organisms.
  • Distinguish between biotic and abiotic factors.
  • Explain habitats, populations, communities and ecosystems.
  • Describe how organisms interact with their environment.
  • Explain environmental conservation and sustainability.

1. Characteristics of Living Organisms

Living organisms carry out life processes that distinguish them from non-living things.

  • Movement: ability to change position or move parts of the body.
  • Respiration: release of energy from food.
  • Sensitivity: ability to detect and respond to stimuli.
  • Growth: permanent increase in size and dry mass.
  • Reproduction: production of new individuals.
  • Excretion: removal of metabolic waste products.
  • Nutrition: obtaining or making substances needed for energy and growth.

2. Levels of Organisation

Organisms can be studied at different levels: cell → tissue → organ → organ system → organism.

3. Habitat

A habitat is the place where an organism normally lives. Examples include a pond, forest, grassland and soil.

4. Biotic and Abiotic Factors

Biotic factors are living influences such as competition, predation, disease and availability of food.

Abiotic factors are non-living environmental factors such as temperature, light intensity, rainfall, pH, soil minerals and water availability.

5. Population, Community and Ecosystem

  • Population: organisms of the same species living in an area.
  • Community: all populations of different species living and interacting in an area.
  • Ecosystem: a community together with its physical environment.

6. Environmental Sustainability

Sustainability means using natural resources in ways that meet present needs without destroying the ability of future generations to meet their needs.

  • Planting and protecting trees.
  • Protecting wildlife habitats.
  • Reducing unnecessary waste.
  • Using water responsibly.
  • Controlling pollution.
  • Using renewable resources where appropriate.

MANEB-Style Practice

  1. State seven characteristics of living organisms.
  2. Differentiate between a population and a community.
  3. Give four abiotic factors affecting organisms.
  4. Explain two ways human activities can damage ecosystems.
  5. Suggest four measures for conserving the environment.

Answers

  1. Movement, respiration, sensitivity, growth, reproduction, excretion and nutrition.
  2. A population consists of one species; a community contains different populations living together.
  3. Temperature, light, rainfall, pH, water availability and mineral content.
  4. Deforestation, pollution, habitat destruction, overfishing and excessive burning can damage ecosystems.
  5. Afforestation, protected areas, proper waste disposal, pollution control and sustainable resource use.

Common Mistakes

  • Do not confuse a habitat with an ecosystem.
  • Do not describe respiration simply as breathing; respiration is a chemical process that releases energy.
  • Remember that abiotic factors are non-living factors.

Examination Tip

When a question asks you to state, give direct points. When it asks you to explain, give the point and its reason or effect.
