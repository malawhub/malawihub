🔬 Cell Structure & Function

Cell organisation, organelles, specialised cells, microscopy and comparison of plant and animal cells.

Learning Objectives

  • Describe the basic structure of cells.
  • State the functions of major cell organelles.
  • Compare plant and animal cells.
  • Explain how cells become specialised.
  • Calculate magnification using microscope measurements.

1. The Cell

The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life. Organisms may consist of one cell or many cells.

2. Animal Cell

  • Cell membrane: controls movement of substances into and out of the cell.
  • Cytoplasm: site of many chemical reactions.
  • Nucleus: contains genetic material and controls cell activities.
  • Mitochondria: site of aerobic respiration and energy release.
  • Ribosomes: site of protein synthesis.

3. Plant Cell

Plant cells contain the structures found in animal cells and additional structures.

  • Cell wall: provides support and strengthens the cell.
  • Chloroplasts: contain chlorophyll and are the main sites of photosynthesis.
  • Large permanent vacuole: contains cell sap and helps maintain turgor.

5. Cell Specialisation

Specialised cells have structures adapted to perform particular functions efficiently.

Root Hair Cell

  • Long extension increases surface area.
  • Thin wall provides a short diffusion distance.
  • Large vacuole helps maintain a water potential gradient.
  • Many mitochondria provide energy for active transport of mineral ions.

Red Blood Cell

  • Biconcave shape provides a large surface area.
  • No nucleus provides more space for haemoglobin.
  • Flexible shape allows movement through narrow capillaries.

Sperm Cell

  • Flagellum provides movement.
  • Many mitochondria provide energy.
  • Acrosome contains enzymes involved in fertilisation.

Guard Cells

Guard cells control the opening and closing of stomata and therefore regulate gas exchange and water loss.

6. Microscopy

A microscope is used to magnify objects that are too small to be seen clearly with the naked eye.

Magnification

Magnification = image size ÷ actual size

All measurements must use the same units before calculation.

Example

An image of a cell is 40 mm long while the actual cell is 0.2 mm long.

Magnification = 40 ÷ 0.2 = ×200

7. Cell Organisation

In multicellular organisms, cells with similar structures and functions form tissues. Tissues form organs, and organs work together in organ systems.

Cell → Tissue → Organ → Organ system → Organism

MANEB-Style Practice

  1. State the function of the nucleus.
  2. Give three structures found in plant cells but not normally found in animal cells.
  3. Explain how the structure of a root hair cell is adapted for absorption.
  4. State two adaptations of a red blood cell.
  5. An image of a cell measures 30 mm and the actual cell measures 0.15 mm. Calculate its magnification.
  6. Differentiate between a tissue and an organ.

Answers

  1. The nucleus contains genetic material and controls cell activities.
  2. Cell wall, chloroplasts and a large permanent vacuole.
  3. Its long extension increases surface area, while its thin wall gives a short diffusion distance. It also has many mitochondria for active transport.
  4. Biconcave shape gives a large surface area; absence of a nucleus provides more space for haemoglobin.
  5. 30 ÷ 0.15 = ×200.
  6. A tissue is a group of similar cells performing a particular function, while an organ consists of different tissues working together.

Common Mistakes

  • Do not say mitochondria produce energy; they are the site of aerobic respiration where energy is released.
  • Do not confuse the cell wall with the cell membrane.
  • Always convert measurements into the same units before calculating magnification.
  • Do not say all plant cells contain chloroplasts; underground cells such as root cells generally do not.

Examination Tips

  • Learn organelle functions accurately.
  • For adaptation questions, always link a structural feature to its function.
  • Show every step when calculating magnification.
  • Practise labelled biological diagrams.
