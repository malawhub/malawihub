❤️ Human Transport & Circulatory System

The heart, blood, blood vessels and transport of substances around the body.

Learning Objectives

  • Explain why humans need a transport system.
  • Describe the structure and functions of the heart.
  • Distinguish between arteries, veins and capillaries.
  • Describe the components and functions of blood.
  • Explain the cardiac cycle and double circulation.
  • Explain how substances are transported around the body.

1. Need for a Transport System

Large multicellular organisms have cells that are far from the external environment. Diffusion alone is too slow to supply all cells with their requirements.

The circulatory system transports substances rapidly between organs and tissues.

2. Main Components

  • Heart
  • Blood
  • Blood vessels

3. The Heart

The heart is a muscular organ that pumps blood around the body.

It has four chambers:

  • Right atrium
  • Right ventricle
  • Left atrium
  • Left ventricle

4. Blood Flow Through the Heart

Deoxygenated blood from the body enters the right atrium through the vena cava.

It passes into the right ventricle and is pumped through the pulmonary artery to the lungs.

Oxygenated blood returns from the lungs through the pulmonary veins into the left atrium.

It then enters the left ventricle and is pumped through the aorta to the rest of the body.

5. Valves

Heart valves prevent the backward flow of blood.

  • Atrioventricular valves are located between the atria and ventricles.
  • Semilunar valves are located at the exits of the ventricles.

6. Septum

The septum separates the left and right sides of the heart.

It prevents oxygenated and deoxygenated blood from mixing directly between the two sides.

7. The Ventricles

The left ventricle has a thicker muscular wall than the right ventricle.

This is because the left ventricle pumps blood around the whole body, while the right ventricle only pumps blood to the lungs.

8. Double Circulation

Humans have a double circulatory system because blood passes through the heart twice during one complete circuit of the body.

Pulmonary Circulation

Blood travels between the heart and lungs.

Right ventricle → pulmonary artery → lungs → pulmonary veins → left atrium

Systemic Circulation

Blood travels between the heart and the rest of the body.

Left ventricle → aorta → body tissues → vena cava → right atrium

9. Arteries

Arteries carry blood away from the heart.

  • Thick muscular walls.
  • Elastic walls.
  • Small lumen relative to wall thickness.
  • Blood is usually under high pressure.

10. Veins

Veins carry blood towards the heart.

  • Thinner walls than arteries.
  • Larger lumen.
  • Lower blood pressure.
  • Valves prevent backflow of blood.

11. Capillaries

Capillaries are tiny blood vessels connecting arteries and veins.

  • Walls are only one cell thick.
  • They form extensive networks around tissues.
  • They allow substances to diffuse between blood and body cells.

12. Comparing Blood Vessels

Feature Artery Vein Capillary
Direction Away from heart Towards heart Connects small arteries and veins
Wall Thick and muscular Thinner One cell thick
Lumen Relatively narrow Relatively wide Very narrow

13. Blood

Blood is a fluid connective tissue that transports substances throughout the body.

Red Blood Cells

  • Transport oxygen.
  • Contain haemoglobin.
  • Have a biconcave shape.
  • Lack a nucleus when mature.

White Blood Cells

  • Defend the body against pathogens.
  • Some engulf microorganisms.
  • Some produce antibodies.
  • Some produce antitoxins.

Platelets

Platelets help blood clot at sites of injury, reducing blood loss and helping prevent entry of microorganisms.

Plasma

Plasma is the liquid part of blood. It transports substances including glucose, amino acids, hormones, carbon dioxide, urea and heat.

14. Haemoglobin

Haemoglobin is a protein in red blood cells that combines reversibly with oxygen.

In the lungs, haemoglobin combines with oxygen to form oxyhaemoglobin. In tissues where oxygen concentration is lower, oxygen is released.

15. Coronary Heart Disease

Coronary heart disease occurs when the blood supply to the heart muscle is reduced or blocked, often because fatty deposits narrow the coronary arteries.

Risk factors include:

  • Smoking
  • Unhealthy diet
  • Lack of physical activity
  • Obesity
  • High blood pressure
  • High blood cholesterol

16. Exercise and the Circulatory System

During exercise, muscles require more oxygen and glucose for respiration.

The heart rate increases so that more blood reaches active muscles. Carbon dioxide and other waste products are also transported away more rapidly.

MANEB-Style Practice

  1. State two functions of the circulatory system.
  2. Name the four chambers of the heart.
  3. State the function of heart valves.
  4. Explain why the left ventricular wall is thicker than the right ventricular wall.
  5. Define double circulation.
  6. State three adaptations of arteries.
  7. State three differences between arteries and veins.
  8. Explain how capillaries are adapted for exchange of substances.
  9. State the functions of red blood cells, white blood cells and platelets.
  10. Explain why heart rate increases during exercise.

Answers

  1. Transport of oxygen, nutrients, hormones and waste products.
  2. Right atrium, right ventricle, left atrium and left ventricle.
  3. They prevent the backward flow of blood.
  4. The left ventricle pumps blood around the whole body and therefore needs to generate higher pressure.
  5. Double circulation is a system in which blood passes through the heart twice during one complete circuit.
  6. Thick muscular walls, elastic walls and a relatively narrow lumen.
  7. Arteries carry blood away from the heart while veins carry blood towards it; arteries have thicker walls; veins have valves and larger lumens.
  8. They have one-cell-thick walls, a large network and short diffusion distances, allowing rapid exchange.
  9. Red blood cells transport oxygen; white blood cells defend against pathogens; platelets help blood clot.
  10. Active muscles need more oxygen and glucose for respiration, so increased heart rate increases blood flow to them.

Common Mistakes

  • Do not define arteries and veins by oxygen content. Define them by direction of blood flow relative to the heart.
  • Remember that the pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood.
  • Remember that the pulmonary veins carry oxygenated blood.
  • Do not confuse plasma with red blood cells.

Examination Tips

  • Practise drawing and labelling the heart.
  • Learn the complete pathway of blood through the heart.
  • Know the structural differences between arteries, veins and capillaries.
  • For adaptation questions, always connect a structural feature with its function.
  • Practise questions involving exercise, heart rate and oxygen supply.
