🌿 Plant Structure & Function

Roots, stems, leaves, plant tissues and structural adaptations.

Learning Objectives

  • Identify the major organs of a flowering plant.
  • Describe the functions of roots, stems and leaves.
  • Explain the functions of major plant tissues.
  • Relate plant structures to their functions.
  • Describe adaptations of leaves, roots and stems.

1. Main Plant Organs

A flowering plant has several organs that work together to keep the plant alive.

  • Roots: anchor the plant and absorb water and mineral ions.
  • Stem: supports leaves and flowers and transports substances.
  • Leaves: carry out photosynthesis and exchange gases.
  • Flowers: reproductive organs involved in sexual reproduction.

2. Root Structure and Function

Roots anchor plants in the soil and absorb water and mineral ions.

Root Hair Cells

  • Long hair-like extension increases surface area.
  • Thin cell wall provides a short pathway for water movement.
  • Large vacuole helps maintain a water potential gradient.
  • Mitochondria provide energy for active transport of mineral ions.

3. Stem Structure and Function

The stem supports leaves and reproductive structures. It also contains vascular tissues for transport.

  • Xylem: transports water and mineral ions mainly upward.
  • Phloem: transports organic assimilates such as sucrose around the plant.
  • Supporting tissues: strengthen stems and help keep leaves exposed to light.

4. Leaf Structure

Leaves are adapted for efficient photosynthesis and gas exchange.

  • Waxy cuticle: reduces water loss.
  • Upper epidermis: protects the leaf.
  • Palisade mesophyll: contains many chloroplasts for photosynthesis.
  • Spongy mesophyll: contains air spaces that allow gases to diffuse.
  • Vascular bundle: contains xylem and phloem.
  • Stomata: allow gas exchange.
  • Guard cells: regulate stomatal opening.

5. Plant Tissues

Xylem

Xylem vessels transport water and mineral ions from the roots. Their walls are strengthened with lignin, providing mechanical support.

Phloem

Phloem transports assimilates such as sucrose from sources to sinks. This movement is called translocation.

Epidermal Tissue

The epidermis forms a protective outer layer. In leaves, the cuticle helps reduce water loss.

6. Plant Adaptations

Desert Plants

  • Reduced leaves or spines.
  • Thick waxy cuticle.
  • Water-storage tissues.
  • Extensive root systems.

Aquatic Plants

  • Large air spaces provide buoyancy.
  • Flexible stems can move with water.
  • Floating leaves may have stomata mainly on the upper surface.

7. Structure and Function

Biological structures are closely related to their functions. For example, a broad leaf provides a large surface area for absorbing light, while numerous stomata allow efficient gas exchange.

MANEB-Style Practice

  1. State three functions of roots.
  2. Explain two adaptations of a root hair cell.
  3. State the function of xylem.
  4. State the function of phloem.
  5. Explain how the palisade mesophyll is adapted for photosynthesis.
  6. Explain why many desert plants have reduced leaves.
  7. Describe the role of guard cells.

Answers

  1. Anchorage, absorption of water and absorption of mineral ions.
  2. Its long extension gives a large surface area, while its thin wall provides a short diffusion distance.
  3. Transport of water and mineral ions and mechanical support.
  4. Transport of assimilates such as sucrose between sources and sinks.
  5. It is near the upper surface where light intensity is high and contains many chloroplasts.
  6. Reducing leaf area decreases water loss through transpiration.
  7. Guard cells control the opening and closing of stomata, regulating gas exchange and water loss.

Common Mistakes

  • Do not say xylem transports food; phloem transports assimilates.
  • Do not confuse transpiration with translocation.
  • Remember that roots absorb mineral ions as well as water.
  • When explaining adaptations, always connect the feature to its advantage.

Examination Tips

  • Practise labelled leaf, root and stem diagrams.
  • Learn the differences between xylem and phloem.
  • For adaptation questions, use the structure → function → advantage approach.
  • Use precise biological terminology in diagram labels.
