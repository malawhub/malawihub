🍎 Human Digestive System

Digestion, digestive organs, enzymes, absorption and assimilation of nutrients.

Learning Objectives

  • Identify the main organs of the human digestive system.
  • Explain mechanical and chemical digestion.
  • Describe the roles of digestive enzymes.
  • Explain absorption of digested food.
  • Explain assimilation of nutrients.
  • Relate digestive-system adaptations to their functions.

1. Digestion

Digestion is the breakdown of large, insoluble food molecules into smaller, soluble molecules that can be absorbed into the bloodstream.

Digestion is both mechanical and chemical.

  • Mechanical digestion: physical breakdown of food into smaller pieces.
  • Chemical digestion: enzymatic breakdown of large molecules into smaller molecules.

2. Main Parts of the Digestive System

Organ Main Function
Mouth Ingestion, chewing and beginning of starch digestion.
Oesophagus Moves food from the mouth to the stomach by peristalsis.
Stomach Stores and churns food; begins protein digestion.
Small intestine Completes digestion and absorbs digested nutrients.
Large intestine Absorbs water and some mineral ions.
Rectum Stores faeces before elimination.
Anus Controls removal of faeces from the body.

3. Mouth

Digestion begins in the mouth.

  • Teeth mechanically break food into smaller pieces.
  • The tongue mixes food with saliva and helps swallowing.
  • Salivary glands release saliva.
  • Saliva contains the enzyme amylase, which begins the digestion of starch.

4. Oesophagus and Peristalsis

The oesophagus is a muscular tube connecting the mouth to the stomach.

Peristalsis is the rhythmic contraction and relaxation of muscles in the gut wall that pushes food along the digestive tract.

5. Stomach

The stomach is a muscular organ that stores and churns food.

Gastric juice contains hydrochloric acid and enzymes.

  • Hydrochloric acid provides a low pH.
  • The acidic environment helps the action of protease enzymes and helps destroy many microorganisms in food.
  • Proteases begin the digestion of proteins into smaller peptides.

6. Liver and Bile

The liver produces bile, which is stored in the gall bladder.

Bile is released into the small intestine.

Functions of Bile

  • Neutralises acidic material entering the small intestine from the stomach.
  • Emulsifies fats into small droplets, increasing the surface area available for lipase.

Important: bile is not an enzyme.

7. Pancreas

The pancreas releases digestive enzymes into the small intestine.

  • Amylase digests starch.
  • Proteases digest proteins.
  • Lipase digests lipids.

8. Small Intestine

The small intestine is the main site of chemical digestion and absorption of digested nutrients.

Its inner surface contains numerous villi, which increase the surface area available for absorption.

Adaptations of Villi

  • Large surface area.
  • Thin epithelial lining.
  • Rich blood supply through capillaries.
  • Lacteals for absorption of many products of lipid digestion.

9. Digestion of Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates such as starch are broken down into simple sugars.

Amylase breaks starch into smaller carbohydrates, which are eventually converted into glucose and other simple sugars by intestinal enzymes.

10. Digestion of Proteins

Proteins are large molecules made from amino acids.

Proteases break proteins into peptides and eventually amino acids.

11. Digestion of Lipids

Lipids are broken down into fatty acids and glycerol.

Bile emulsifies fats, while lipase catalyses the chemical digestion of lipids.

12. Absorption

Absorption is the movement of digested, soluble food molecules from the small intestine into the blood or lymph.

  • Glucose and amino acids enter blood capillaries.
  • Many products of lipid digestion enter the lacteals and are transported through the lymphatic system before entering the bloodstream.

13. Assimilation

Assimilation is the use of absorbed nutrients by cells and tissues.

  • Glucose can be used in respiration to release energy.
  • Amino acids are used to build proteins.
  • Fatty acids and glycerol can be used to form stored lipids and other substances.

14. Large Intestine

The large intestine absorbs water and some mineral ions from undigested material.

The remaining material forms faeces, which are stored temporarily in the rectum and removed through the anus.

15. Summary of Digestive Enzymes

Enzyme Substrate Products
Amylase Starch Smaller carbohydrates
Protease Proteins Peptides/amino acids
Lipase Lipids Fatty acids and glycerol

MANEB-Style Practice

  1. Define digestion.
  2. State the function of the oesophagus.
  3. What is peristalsis?
  4. State two functions of hydrochloric acid in the stomach.
  5. Explain the role of bile in fat digestion.
  6. Why are villi important in the small intestine?
  7. State the products of protein digestion.
  8. State the products of lipid digestion.
  9. Differentiate between absorption and assimilation.
  10. Explain three adaptations of the small intestine for absorption.

Answers

  1. Digestion is the breakdown of large insoluble food molecules into smaller soluble molecules.
  2. It transports food from the mouth to the stomach by peristalsis.
  3. Peristalsis is the rhythmic muscular movement that pushes food along the digestive tract.
  4. It provides a low pH for protease activity and helps destroy many microorganisms.
  5. Bile neutralises acidic contents and emulsifies fats into small droplets, increasing the surface area for lipase action.
  6. Villi greatly increase the surface area for absorption.
  7. Peptides and eventually amino acids.
  8. Fatty acids and glycerol.
  9. Absorption is movement of digested nutrients into blood or lymph; assimilation is their use by cells and tissues.
  10. Large surface area, thin lining and rich blood supply are examples.

Common Mistakes

  • Do not call bile an enzyme.
  • Do not confuse absorption with assimilation.
  • Remember that digestion starts in the mouth.
  • Do not say the stomach completes all digestion.
  • Distinguish mechanical digestion from chemical digestion.

Examination Tips

  • Learn the functions of each major digestive organ.
  • Memorise the main digestive enzymes, their substrates and products.
  • Be able to explain how villi are adapted for absorption.
  • For enzyme questions, always identify the substrate and product.
  • Practise labelled digestive-system diagrams.
