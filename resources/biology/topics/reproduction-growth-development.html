Reproduction, Growth & Development

Human reproduction, fertilisation, pregnancy, birth, development and reproductive health.

Learning Objectives

  • Distinguish between sexual and asexual reproduction.
  • Describe the male and female reproductive systems.
  • Explain the menstrual cycle and ovulation.
  • Explain fertilisation and implantation.
  • Describe development of the embryo and fetus.
  • Explain the role of the placenta.
  • Describe puberty and reproductive hormones.
  • Explain methods of contraception.

1. Reproduction

Reproduction is the biological process by which organisms produce new individuals of their own species.

Sexual Reproduction

Sexual reproduction involves the fusion of male and female gametes. The offspring receive genetic material from two parents and are genetically different from their parents and from one another.

Asexual Reproduction

Asexual reproduction involves one parent and does not involve the fusion of gametes. The offspring are usually genetically very similar to the parent.

2. Male Reproductive System

Structure Function
Testes Produce sperm and testosterone.
Scrotum Holds the testes outside the main body cavity and helps maintain a suitable temperature for sperm production.
Epididymis Site where sperm mature and are stored.
Sperm duct Transports sperm towards the urethra.
Seminal vesicles and prostate gland Add fluids to sperm to form semen.
Urethra Carries semen and urine out of the body at different times.
Penis Transfers semen into the female reproductive tract.

3. Female Reproductive System

Structure Function
Ovaries Produce ova and female reproductive hormones.
Oviducts Carry ova towards the uterus; fertilisation usually occurs here.
Uterus Contains and supports the developing embryo and fetus.
Uterine lining Becomes thick and well supplied with blood in preparation for implantation.
Cervix Lower opening of the uterus.
Vagina Receives the penis and sperm and forms the birth canal.

4. Gametes

Sperm Cell

  • Small and adapted for movement.
  • Has a flagellum for swimming.
  • Contains many mitochondria to provide energy.
  • Contains enzymes in the acrosome that help it penetrate the egg.
  • Contains a haploid nucleus.

Egg Cell

  • Larger than a sperm cell.
  • Contains stored nutrients for early development.
  • Has a protective outer layer.
  • Contains a haploid nucleus.

5. Puberty

Puberty is the period during which a child develops reproductive maturity.

Hormonal changes cause the reproductive organs to mature and produce secondary sexual characteristics.

Examples in Males

  • Growth of the testes and penis.
  • Production of sperm.
  • Deepening of the voice.
  • Growth of facial and body hair.

Examples in Females

  • Development of breasts.
  • Beginning of menstruation.
  • Widening of the hips.
  • Growth of pubic and underarm hair.

6. Menstrual Cycle

The menstrual cycle prepares the female reproductive system for possible pregnancy.

During the cycle, an egg develops in an ovary and the uterine lining becomes thicker.

Ovulation

Ovulation is the release of a mature egg from an ovary.

Ovulation occurs approximately midway through a typical 28-day cycle, although cycle length varies between individuals.

7. Reproductive Hormones

Hormone Main Role
FSH Stimulates development of ovarian follicles and promotes sperm production in males.
LH Triggers ovulation and supports formation of the corpus luteum.
Oestrogen Promotes development of female secondary sexual characteristics and helps rebuild the uterine lining.
Progesterone Maintains the uterine lining after ovulation.
Testosterone Promotes male reproductive development and secondary sexual characteristics.

8. Fertilisation

Fertilisation is the fusion of the nuclei of a male gamete and a female gamete.

In humans, fertilisation usually occurs in the oviduct.

The fertilised egg forms a zygote, which begins repeated mitotic divisions.

9. Implantation

As the developing embryo moves towards the uterus, it becomes a ball of cells known as a blastocyst.

Implantation occurs when the developing embryo becomes attached to and embedded in the lining of the uterus.

10. Placenta

The placenta develops during pregnancy and forms an exchange surface between the maternal blood supply and the fetal circulation.

Substances such as oxygen and glucose pass from the mother to the fetus, while carbon dioxide and urea pass from the fetus towards the mother's blood for removal.

Maternal and fetal blood normally do not mix directly.

11. Umbilical Cord

The umbilical cord connects the fetus to the placenta.

It contains blood vessels that transport substances between the fetus and placenta.

12. Fetal Protection

The fetus is protected by several structures and processes.

  • The amniotic fluid cushions the fetus against physical shocks.
  • The uterus provides a protected environment for development.
  • The placenta provides an exchange surface and also contributes to hormonal support of pregnancy.

13. Birth

Near the end of pregnancy, hormonal changes stimulate contractions of the uterus.

Strong uterine contractions help push the baby through the cervix and vagina.

14. Growth and Development

Growth is a permanent increase in size or dry mass.

Development refers to progressive changes in form, structure and function as an organism becomes more mature.

Human growth and development depend on genetic factors, hormones, nutrition, health and environmental conditions.

15. Contraception

Contraception refers to methods used to reduce the probability of pregnancy.

Examples

  • Condoms
  • Oral contraceptive pills
  • Implants
  • Injectable contraceptives
  • Intrauterine devices
  • Fertility-awareness methods
  • Sterilisation

Condoms have the additional advantage of reducing the risk of transmission of many sexually transmitted infections.

16. Sexually Transmitted Infections

Sexually transmitted infections are infections that can be transmitted through sexual contact.

Examples include HIV, gonorrhoea, syphilis and chlamydia.

Reducing risk involves safer sexual practices, appropriate testing, treatment where available and informed reproductive-health decisions.

MANEB-Style Practice

  1. Define sexual reproduction.
  2. State two functions of the testes.
  3. State the function of the ovaries.
  4. Where does fertilisation usually occur in humans?
  5. Define ovulation.
  6. State two functions of progesterone.
  7. Explain how a sperm cell is adapted for fertilisation.
  8. Explain the importance of the placenta.
  9. Distinguish between growth and development.
  10. State three methods of contraception.
  11. Explain one advantage of condoms in addition to preventing pregnancy.

Answers

  1. Sexual reproduction involves the fusion of male and female gametes to produce genetically varied offspring.
  2. The testes produce sperm and testosterone.
  3. The ovaries produce ova and female reproductive hormones.
  4. Usually in the oviduct.
  5. Ovulation is the release of a mature egg from an ovary.
  6. Progesterone maintains the uterine lining and supports conditions suitable for pregnancy.
  7. It has a flagellum for movement, many mitochondria for energy and an acrosome containing enzymes that help it penetrate the egg.
  8. The placenta provides an exchange surface between maternal and fetal blood supplies and allows nutrients and oxygen to reach the fetus while wastes are removed.
  9. Growth is a permanent increase in size or dry mass, while development involves progressive changes in form, structure and function.
  10. Examples include condoms, pills, implants, injections and intrauterine devices.
  11. Condoms can reduce the risk of transmission of many sexually transmitted infections.

Common Mistakes

  • Do not confuse fertilisation with implantation.
  • Remember that fertilisation usually occurs in the oviduct, not the uterus.
  • Do not say that maternal and fetal blood normally mix directly in the placenta.
  • Distinguish ovulation from menstruation.

Examination Tips

  • Practise labelled diagrams of the male and female reproductive systems.
  • Learn the functions of the reproductive hormones.
  • Be able to explain fertilisation, implantation and fetal development in sequence.
  • For placenta questions, mention exchange of oxygen, nutrients and waste products.
  • Know several methods of contraception and their main functions.
