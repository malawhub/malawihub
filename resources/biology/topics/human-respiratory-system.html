Human Respiratory System

Gas exchange, breathing, ventilation and aerobic respiration.

Learning Objectives

  • Identify the main parts of the human respiratory system.
  • Explain the functions of the respiratory organs.
  • Describe the mechanism of breathing.
  • Explain gaseous exchange in the alveoli.
  • Explain how exercise affects breathing rate.
  • Describe factors that affect respiratory health.

1. Respiration and Breathing

Respiration is the chemical process by which cells release energy from food.

Aerobic respiration uses oxygen and releases energy from glucose.

Breathing, or ventilation, is the movement of air into and out of the lungs.

2. Main Parts of the Respiratory System

Part Function
Nose and nasal cavity Filters, warms and moistens incoming air.
Trachea Carries air towards the bronchi.
Bronchi Carry air into the right and left lungs.
Bronchioles Small air passages distributing air through the lungs.
Alveoli Sites of gaseous exchange.
Diaphragm Changes thoracic volume during breathing.
Intercostal muscles Move the ribs during ventilation.

3. Air Passage

During inhalation, air normally follows this pathway:

Nose or mouth → trachea → bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli

4. Adaptations of the Trachea

  • C-shaped rings of cartilage help prevent the trachea from collapsing.
  • The lining produces mucus that traps dust and microorganisms.
  • Cilia move mucus towards the throat.

5. Bronchi and Bronchioles

The trachea divides into two main bronchi, one entering each lung.

The bronchi branch repeatedly into smaller bronchioles, distributing air throughout the lungs.

6. Alveoli

Alveoli are tiny air sacs where oxygen and carbon dioxide are exchanged between the air and the blood.

Adaptations of Alveoli

  • They occur in very large numbers, providing a large surface area.
  • The walls are very thin, giving a short diffusion distance.
  • They have a rich network of blood capillaries.
  • The moist surface allows gases to dissolve before diffusing.

7. Gaseous Exchange

Oxygen concentration is higher in the air inside the alveoli than in the blood arriving at the lungs. Oxygen therefore diffuses from the alveoli into the blood.

Carbon dioxide concentration is higher in the blood arriving at the lungs than in the alveolar air. Carbon dioxide therefore diffuses from the blood into the alveoli and is breathed out.

8. Inhalation

During inhalation:

  1. The diaphragm contracts and flattens.
  2. The external intercostal muscles contract.
  3. The ribs move upwards and outwards.
  4. The volume of the thoracic cavity increases.
  5. Pressure inside the lungs decreases.
  6. Air moves into the lungs.

9. Exhalation

During normal exhalation:

  1. The diaphragm relaxes and becomes more dome-shaped.
  2. The external intercostal muscles relax.
  3. The ribs move downwards and inwards.
  4. The volume of the thoracic cavity decreases.
  5. Pressure inside the lungs increases.
  6. Air moves out of the lungs.

10. Ventilation

Ventilation is the continuous movement of air into and out of the lungs.

Efficient ventilation maintains concentration gradients between alveolar air and the blood, allowing gaseous exchange to continue.

11. Effects of Exercise

During exercise, muscles require more energy. The rate of aerobic respiration increases, increasing the demand for oxygen and production of carbon dioxide.

As a result:

  • Breathing rate increases.
  • Depth of breathing increases.
  • More oxygen reaches the blood.
  • More carbon dioxide is removed.

12. Measuring Breathing Rate

Breathing rate can be measured by counting the number of breaths taken in a given period.

For example, if a person takes 18 breaths in one minute, the breathing rate is 18 breaths per minute.

13. Respiratory Health

The respiratory system can be damaged by harmful substances and infections.

Smoking

Smoking exposes the respiratory system to harmful chemicals. It can damage cilia, irritate airways and increase the risk of serious diseases including lung cancer and chronic respiratory disease.

Air Pollution

Dust, smoke and other pollutants can irritate the respiratory tract and worsen respiratory conditions.

14. Aerobic Respiration

The overall word equation for aerobic respiration is:

glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy

The energy released is used for processes such as muscle contraction, active transport, growth and maintaining body temperature.

MANEB-Style Practice

  1. Define breathing.
  2. State the function of the trachea.
  3. State two adaptations of alveoli for gaseous exchange.
  4. Explain why oxygen diffuses from the alveoli into the blood.
  5. Describe the events that occur during inhalation.
  6. Describe the events that occur during normal exhalation.
  7. Explain why breathing rate increases during exercise.
  8. State two harmful effects of smoking on the respiratory system.
  9. Write the word equation for aerobic respiration.
  10. Explain why efficient ventilation is important for gaseous exchange.

Answers

  1. Breathing is the movement of air into and out of the lungs.
  2. It carries air towards the bronchi and is supported by cartilage to prevent collapse.
  3. Large surface area, thin walls, moist surface and rich blood supply are examples.
  4. There is a concentration gradient, with a higher oxygen concentration in the alveoli than in the blood arriving at the lungs.
  5. The diaphragm contracts and flattens, intercostal muscles contract, ribs move upwards and outwards, thoracic volume increases, pressure decreases and air enters the lungs.
  6. The diaphragm and intercostal muscles relax, thoracic volume decreases, pressure increases and air leaves the lungs.
  7. Muscles need more energy during exercise, increasing oxygen demand and carbon dioxide production.
  8. Smoking damages cilia and increases the risk of respiratory diseases such as lung cancer.
  9. Glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy.
  10. Ventilation refreshes the air in the alveoli and helps maintain concentration gradients for diffusion.

Common Mistakes

  • Do not confuse breathing with respiration.
  • Do not say that oxygen is actively pumped into the blood at the alveoli.
  • Remember that gaseous exchange occurs mainly in the alveoli.
  • When explaining inhalation, include changes in volume and pressure.

Examination Tips

  • Learn the pathway taken by air.
  • Practise labelled diagrams of the respiratory system.
  • For ventilation questions, explain the sequence of muscle movement, rib movement, volume change, pressure change and air movement.
  • For alveoli questions, remember surface area, thin walls, moisture and blood supply.
