☀️ Plant Nutrition & Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis, chlorophyll, mineral nutrients and factors affecting photosynthesis.

Learning Objectives

  • Explain why plants need nutrients.
  • Define photosynthesis and state its importance.
  • Describe the role of chlorophyll and chloroplasts.
  • Explain the effects of light intensity, carbon dioxide and temperature on photosynthesis.
  • Describe mineral requirements of plants.
  • Interpret simple photosynthesis experiments and graphs.

1. Plant Nutrition

Plants need mineral ions, water, carbon dioxide and light to grow and carry out essential processes.

Green plants are mainly autotrophic because they manufacture organic food from inorganic substances during photosynthesis.

2. Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants use light energy to manufacture glucose from carbon dioxide and water, releasing oxygen as a by-product.

Carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen

Light energy and chlorophyll are required.

3. Importance of Photosynthesis

  • Produces glucose used in respiration.
  • Provides the starting material for making other organic substances.
  • Releases oxygen into the atmosphere.
  • Forms the basis of most food chains.
  • Stores solar energy as chemical energy in organic compounds.

4. Chlorophyll and Chloroplasts

Chlorophyll is the green pigment that absorbs light energy. Chloroplasts contain chlorophyll and are the main sites of photosynthesis in green plant cells.

5. What Happens to Glucose?

Glucose produced during photosynthesis may be:

  • Used immediately in respiration.
  • Converted to starch for storage.
  • Used to make cellulose for cell walls.
  • Combined with mineral ions to form amino acids and proteins.
  • Converted into other substances needed by the plant.

6. Limiting Factors

A limiting factor is a factor that prevents the rate of a process from increasing further when other factors are favourable.

Light Intensity

At low light intensity, increasing light intensity generally increases the rate of photosynthesis. Eventually another factor becomes limiting and the rate levels off.

Carbon Dioxide Concentration

Increasing carbon dioxide concentration can increase the rate of photosynthesis until another factor becomes limiting.

Temperature

Photosynthesis involves enzyme-controlled reactions. Increasing temperature may increase the rate up to an optimum. Above the optimum, enzyme activity is reduced and photosynthesis slows.

7. Mineral Requirements

Nitrate Ions

Nitrate ions are needed to make amino acids and proteins.

Deficiency: poor growth and yellowing of older leaves.

Magnesium Ions

Magnesium is required for chlorophyll formation.

Deficiency: yellowing of leaves due to reduced chlorophyll production.

Phosphate Ions

Phosphate is important in ATP, nucleic acids and cell membranes.

Potassium Ions

Potassium is important for enzyme activity and healthy plant growth.

8. Testing a Leaf for Starch

Starch is commonly used as evidence that photosynthesis has occurred.

  1. Destarch the plant by keeping it in darkness for a suitable period.
  2. Expose the plant to the required experimental conditions.
  3. Remove a leaf.
  4. Boil the leaf briefly in water.
  5. Heat it in ethanol using a water bath to remove chlorophyll.
  6. Rinse the leaf in warm water to soften it.
  7. Add iodine solution.

A blue-black colour indicates the presence of starch.

9. Investigating Light and Photosynthesis

An aquatic plant can be used to investigate the effect of light intensity by measuring oxygen production.

  • Place the plant in water under controlled conditions.
  • Change the distance between the plant and a light source.
  • Measure oxygen production over equal time intervals.
  • Repeat measurements.
  • Calculate averages.
  • Plot a graph of the measured rate against light intensity or distance.

10. Factors to Control

  • Temperature.
  • Carbon dioxide availability.
  • Plant size or species.
  • Time allowed for each measurement.
  • Volume of water.

MANEB-Style Practice

  1. Define photosynthesis.
  2. Write the word equation for photosynthesis.
  3. State two functions of glucose in plants.
  4. Explain why increasing light intensity does not increase photosynthesis indefinitely.
  5. State the role of magnesium in plants.
  6. Describe how to test a leaf for starch.
  7. Explain why a plant is destarched before a photosynthesis experiment.
  8. State two factors that should be controlled when investigating light intensity and photosynthesis.

Answers

  1. Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants use light energy to manufacture glucose from carbon dioxide and water.
  2. Carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen.
  3. Respiration, starch storage, cellulose production and formation of other organic compounds.
  4. Eventually another factor, such as carbon dioxide concentration or temperature, becomes limiting.
  5. Magnesium is needed for chlorophyll formation.
  6. Destarch the plant, remove the leaf, boil it, remove chlorophyll using ethanol in a water bath, rinse it and add iodine. Blue-black indicates starch.
  7. To remove previously stored starch so that newly formed starch can be linked to the experimental conditions.
  8. Temperature and carbon dioxide concentration are examples.

Common Mistakes

  • Do not say plants obtain food directly from the soil; mineral ions and water are absorbed from the soil, while organic food is manufactured by photosynthesis.
  • Do not confuse chlorophyll with chloroplasts.
  • Remember that oxygen is a product of photosynthesis.
  • In experiments, explain why the plant is destarched before testing.

Examination Tips

  • Know the photosynthesis word equation.
  • Learn the functions and deficiency symptoms of important mineral ions.
  • Practise interpreting limiting-factor graphs.
  • For practical questions, explain the purpose of each experimental step.
