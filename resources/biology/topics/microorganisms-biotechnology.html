Microorganisms & Biotechnology

Microorganisms, their uses, harmful effects, fermentation, genetic engineering and biotechnology.

Learning Objectives

  • Define a microorganism.
  • Identify major groups of microorganisms.
  • Describe useful and harmful effects of microorganisms.
  • Explain fermentation.
  • Describe commercial uses of microorganisms.
  • Explain biotechnology and genetic engineering.
  • Describe applications of biotechnology in agriculture, medicine and industry.
  • Evaluate advantages and possible risks of biotechnology.

1. Microorganisms

Microorganisms are organisms or infectious agents that are too small to be seen clearly with the unaided eye.

Important groups include bacteria, fungi, protozoa and microscopic algae. Viruses are also microscopic infectious agents, although they are not considered cellular organisms.

2. Bacteria

Bacteria are single-celled prokaryotic organisms. They occur in soil, water, air, food and living organisms.

Useful Bacteria

  • Decompose dead organic matter.
  • Recycle nutrients in ecosystems.
  • Help in nitrogen cycling.
  • Are used in production of yoghurt and other fermented foods.
  • Can be used to produce useful chemicals and medicines.

Harmful Bacteria

Some bacteria cause diseases in humans, animals and plants. Others spoil food or produce toxins.

3. Fungi

Fungi include microscopic organisms such as yeasts and moulds.

Yeast

Yeast is a single-celled fungus commonly used in fermentation.

Moulds

Some moulds are useful in food production and medicine, while others cause food spoilage or disease.

4. Protozoa

Protozoa are mostly single-celled eukaryotic organisms.

Some protozoa are harmless or useful, while others cause diseases. For example, Plasmodium causes malaria.

5. Viruses

Viruses are microscopic infectious agents that contain genetic material surrounded by a protein coat. They reproduce only inside suitable host cells.

Examples of viral diseases include influenza, measles and HIV infection.

6. Decomposition

Microorganisms such as bacteria and fungi act as decomposers.

They break down dead organisms and waste materials into simpler substances. This returns nutrients to the environment and supports nutrient cycles.

7. Fermentation

Fermentation is a metabolic process carried out by microorganisms under conditions where oxygen is limited or absent.

Alcoholic Fermentation

Yeast converts glucose into ethanol and carbon dioxide.

Word equation: glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide + energy

Uses

  • Bread production.
  • Production of alcoholic beverages.
  • Some industrial processes.

8. Bread Making

Yeast is added to dough containing sugars.

Yeast respires anaerobically and produces carbon dioxide. The carbon dioxide forms bubbles that make the dough rise.

9. Yoghurt Production

Selected bacteria ferment lactose in milk to produce lactic acid.

The acid changes the properties of milk proteins and gives yoghurt its characteristic texture and taste.

10. Industrial Biotechnology

Industrial biotechnology uses living organisms, cells or biological molecules to produce useful products.

Microorganisms can be grown in controlled conditions in large vessels called fermenters or bioreactors.

Conditions Controlled in a Fermenter

  • Temperature
  • pH
  • Oxygen concentration where required
  • Nutrient supply
  • Agitation
  • Sterility

11. Production of Antibiotics

Some microorganisms produce substances that inhibit or kill other microorganisms.

These substances can be developed and manufactured as antibiotics for treating bacterial infections.

12. Biotechnology

Biotechnology is the application of biological organisms, cells or biological processes to produce useful products or solve practical problems.

Examples include fermentation, production of medicines, crop improvement, tissue culture and genetic engineering.

13. Genetic Engineering

Genetic engineering involves deliberately modifying an organism's genetic material to introduce, remove or alter particular characteristics.

General Process

  1. Identify a gene associated with a desired characteristic.
  2. Obtain the required DNA.
  3. Insert the gene into a suitable vector or DNA molecule.
  4. Transfer the genetic material into a host cell.
  5. Select cells that have received the desired genetic material.
  6. Allow the cells to multiply and express the desired characteristic.

14. Genetically Modified Organisms

A genetically modified organism, or GMO, has had its genetic material deliberately altered using biotechnology.

Possible Agricultural Benefits

  • Improved crop resistance to pests.
  • Improved resistance to some diseases.
  • Improved nutritional characteristics.
  • Potential improvement of crop yield or quality.

Possible Concerns

  • Environmental effects must be carefully assessed.
  • Long-term ecological effects require monitoring.
  • There may be social, economic and ethical concerns.

15. Biotechnology in Medicine

Biotechnology can be used to produce medicines and other medical products.

Examples include production of some hormones, vaccines, diagnostic materials and therapeutic proteins.

16. Insulin Production

Genetic engineering can be used to produce human insulin using genetically modified microorganisms.

The human insulin gene can be introduced into suitable cells, which are then grown under controlled conditions to produce insulin.

17. Tissue Culture

Plant tissue culture involves growing plant cells or tissues under sterile conditions on a nutrient medium.

Uses

  • Rapid multiplication of plants.
  • Production of disease-free planting material.
  • Conservation of valuable plant varieties.
  • Propagation of plants that are difficult to reproduce by conventional methods.

18. Biotechnology in Agriculture

Biotechnology can help improve crops and livestock through methods such as selective breeding, tissue culture and genetic engineering.

Applications may include disease resistance, pest resistance, improved nutritional quality and production of planting material.

19. Environmental Biotechnology

Microorganisms can be used to break down organic pollutants and wastes.

Examples include sewage treatment, composting and biological treatment of some contaminated environments.

20. Advantages of Microorganisms

  • Decomposition and nutrient recycling.
  • Food production.
  • Medicine production.
  • Waste treatment.
  • Industrial production.
  • Biological research.

21. Harmful Effects

  • Cause infectious diseases.
  • Cause food spoilage.
  • Produce toxins.
  • Damage crops and livestock.
  • Can contaminate water and food.

MANEB-Style Practice

  1. Define a microorganism.
  2. Name four groups of microorganisms.
  3. State two useful roles of bacteria.
  4. Explain the role of yeast in bread making.
  5. Write the word equation for alcoholic fermentation.
  6. What is biotechnology?
  7. Define genetic engineering.
  8. State three applications of biotechnology in medicine.
  9. Explain one use of tissue culture.
  10. State two possible benefits of genetically modified crops.
  11. State two possible concerns associated with genetic modification.

Answers

  1. A microorganism is an organism or infectious agent that is too small to be seen clearly with the unaided eye.
  2. Bacteria, fungi, protozoa and microscopic algae. Viruses are also microscopic infectious agents.
  3. They decompose organic matter and can be used in food production.
  4. Yeast ferments sugars in the dough and produces carbon dioxide, which makes the dough rise.
  5. Glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide + energy.
  6. Biotechnology is the application of organisms, cells or biological processes to produce useful products or solve practical problems.
  7. Genetic engineering is the deliberate modification of an organism's genetic material to introduce, remove or alter characteristics.
  8. Production of medicines, vaccines, diagnostic materials and therapeutic proteins.
  9. Tissue culture can rapidly produce large numbers of plants from small pieces of plant tissue.
  10. Improved pest resistance and improved nutritional quality are examples.
  11. Possible environmental effects and social, economic or ethical concerns.

Common Mistakes

  • Do not describe viruses as ordinary cells.
  • Do not confuse fermentation with aerobic respiration.
  • Remember that antibiotics are mainly used against bacterial infections, not viral infections.
  • Do not assume that every microorganism is harmful.

Examination Tips

  • Know examples of useful and harmful microorganisms.
  • Practise fermentation equations and applications.
  • Be able to explain biotechnology using clear biological steps.
  • For genetic engineering questions, describe the transfer of genetic material and selection of successful cells.
  • When evaluating biotechnology, discuss both benefits and possible risks.
