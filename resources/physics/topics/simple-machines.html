Simple Machines

Physics • Secondary School • MANEB-focused study notes

1. Introduction

A machine is a device that makes work easier by changing the size or direction of a force. Simple machines include levers, pulleys, gears, inclined planes, wheels and axles, and screws.

2. Learning Objectives

  • Explain the purpose of simple machines.
  • Define load, effort and mechanical advantage.
  • Calculate mechanical advantage and efficiency.
  • Explain velocity ratio.
  • Describe common simple machines and their applications.
  • Solve examination-style problems.

3. Load and Effort

Load: The force or resistance that the machine is used to overcome.
Effort: The force applied to the machine.

Machines do not create energy. They make tasks easier by allowing a smaller effort to move a larger load, often over a greater distance.

4. Mechanical Advantage

\(MA=\frac{\text{Load}}{\text{Effort}}\)
Example: A machine lifts a 600 N load using an effort of 150 N. Find its mechanical advantage.
\[ MA=\frac{600}{150}=\boxed{4} \]

A mechanical advantage of 4 means the load is four times the effort.

5. Velocity Ratio

Velocity ratio (VR) is the ratio of the distance moved by the effort to the distance moved by the load.
\(VR=\frac{\text{distance moved by effort}}{\text{distance moved by load}}\)
Example: The effort moves 2 m while the load moves 0.5 m.
\[ VR=\frac{2}{0.5}=\boxed{4} \]

6. Efficiency

\[ \text{Efficiency}=\frac{MA}{VR}\times100\% \]

A real machine is less than 100% efficient because some energy is lost, mainly through friction, heat and sound.

Example: A machine has MA = 3 and VR = 4. Calculate its efficiency.
\[ \text{Efficiency}=\frac{3}{4}\times100=\boxed{75\%} \]

7. Levers

A lever is a rigid bar that turns about a fixed point called a fulcrum.

ClassArrangementExample
First-classFulcrum between effort and loadScissors, seesaw
Second-classLoad between fulcrum and effortWheelbarrow
Third-classEffort between fulcrum and loadHuman forearm, tweezers
The position of the fulcrum, effort and load determines the class of lever.

8. Pulley

A pulley is a wheel with a groove over which a rope or belt passes.

Pulleys can change the direction of an applied force and, in systems with several supporting rope sections, reduce the effort required to lift a load.

Example: A fixed pulley may allow a person to pull downward while the load moves upward. Its major advantage is changing the direction of the effort.

9. Inclined Plane

An inclined plane is a sloping surface used to raise a load through a vertical height using a smaller force over a longer distance.

Examples include ramps, sloping roads and loading platforms.

A longer, gentler ramp generally requires less effort, but the effort acts through a greater distance.

10. Wheel and Axle

A wheel and axle consists of a large wheel attached to a smaller axle. Turning one causes the other to rotate.

Examples include steering wheels, door handles and some hand-operated machines.

11. Gears

Gears are toothed wheels that transmit rotational motion.

  • Meshing gears rotate in opposite directions.
  • A small driving gear can make a larger driven gear rotate more slowly.
  • A large driving gear can make a smaller driven gear rotate faster.

Gears are used in bicycles, clocks, vehicles and machinery.

12. Screw

A screw is an inclined plane wrapped around a cylinder.

It can convert rotational motion into forward motion and provide mechanical advantage.

Examples include screws, screw jacks and some lifting devices.

13. Work Input and Work Output

\(W=Fd\)

For a machine:

\[ \text{Work input}=\text{effort}\times\text{distance moved by effort} \] \[ \text{Work output}=\text{load}\times\text{distance moved by load} \]
\[ \text{Efficiency}=\frac{\text{work output}}{\text{work input}}\times100\% \]

14. MANEB Examination Focus

  • Define a simple machine.
  • Identify classes of levers.
  • Calculate MA, VR and efficiency.
  • Explain why machines cannot be 100% efficient in practice.
  • Describe applications of pulleys, gears, levers and inclined planes.
  • Explain the relationship between effort distance and load distance.

15. MANEB-Style Practice

1. Define mechanical advantage.
2. A machine lifts a 900 N load with an effort of 300 N. Calculate MA.
3. The effort moves 3 m while the load moves 0.75 m. Calculate VR.
4. A machine has MA = 2.4 and VR = 3. Calculate its efficiency.
5. State the class of lever represented by a wheelbarrow.
6. Explain one advantage of using an inclined plane.

16. Answers

  1. Load divided by effort.
  2. \(MA=900/300=\boxed{3}\).
  3. \(VR=3/0.75=\boxed{4}\).
  4. \(\text{Efficiency}=(2.4/3)\times100=\boxed{80\%}\).
  5. Second-class lever.
  6. It allows a load to be raised using a smaller effort over a longer distance.

17. Common Mistakes

  • Confusing MA with VR.
  • Forgetting to multiply efficiency by 100.
  • Using the load as the effort.
  • Confusing the three classes of lever.
  • Assuming a machine produces energy.

18. Examination Tips

  • Write the formula before substituting values.
  • Keep load and effort clearly identified.
  • Show all calculation steps.
  • Include percentage units for efficiency.
  • For lever questions, identify the fulcrum first.
