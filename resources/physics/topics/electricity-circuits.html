Electricity and Circuits

Physics • Secondary School • MANEB-focused study notes

1. Introduction

Electricity is associated with the movement and behaviour of electric charges. Electrical circuits allow electrical energy to be transferred and used in devices such as lamps, motors, heaters, radios and electronic equipment.

2. Learning Objectives

  • Explain electric charge and electric current.
  • Define potential difference and resistance.
  • Use the correct electrical units.
  • Apply Ohm's law.
  • Distinguish series and parallel circuits.
  • Calculate resistance and electrical power.
  • Explain electrical energy consumption.
  • Use ammeters and voltmeters correctly.
  • Explain household electrical safety.

3. Electric Charge

Electric charge is a property of matter responsible for electrical effects.

There are two types of charge:

  • positive charge;
  • negative charge.

Like charges repel each other, while unlike charges attract each other.

Electric charge is measured in coulombs (C).

4. Electric Current

Electric current is the rate of flow of electric charge through a conductor.
\[ I=\frac{Q}{t} \]

where:

  • \(I\) = current in amperes (A)
  • \(Q\) = charge in coulombs (C)
  • \(t\) = time in seconds (s)
Example: A charge of 60 C passes through a conductor in 20 s. Calculate the current.
\[ I=\frac{Q}{t} \] \[ I=\frac{60}{20} \] \[ \boxed{I=3\text{ A}} \]

5. Conventional Current

Conventional current is taken to flow from the positive terminal of a source through the external circuit towards the negative terminal.

In metallic conductors, electrons actually move in the opposite direction.

Do not confuse the direction of conventional current with the direction of electron movement.

6. Potential Difference

Potential difference (p.d.) between two points is the work done or energy transferred per unit charge when charge moves between those points.
\[ V=\frac{W}{Q} \]

where:

  • \(V\) = potential difference in volts (V)
  • \(W\) = energy transferred in joules (J)
  • \(Q\) = charge in coulombs (C)
A device transfers 120 J of energy when 20 C of charge passes through it. Calculate the p.d.
\[ V=\frac{120}{20} \] \[ \boxed{V=6\text{ V}} \]

7. Resistance

Resistance is the opposition to the flow of electric current.

Resistance is measured in ohms (Ω).

Resistance of a wire depends on factors such as:

  • length;
  • cross-sectional area;
  • material;
  • temperature.

For a uniform wire, resistance generally increases as length increases and decreases as cross-sectional area increases.

8. Ohm's Law

For a conductor at constant temperature, the current through it is directly proportional to the potential difference across it.
\[ V=IR \]

Therefore:

\[ I=\frac{V}{R} \qquad R=\frac{V}{I} \]
A 12 V supply is connected across a resistor of 4 Ω. Calculate the current.
\[ I=\frac{V}{R} \] \[ I=\frac{12}{4} \] \[ \boxed{I=3\text{ A}} \]

10. Ammeter and Voltmeter

Ammeter: Instrument used to measure current.

An ammeter is connected in series with the component.

Voltmeter: Instrument used to measure potential difference.

A voltmeter is connected in parallel across the component.

Memory rule: Ammeter → series. Voltmeter → parallel.

11. Series Circuits

In a series circuit, components are connected along one path.

Important properties:

  • The same current flows through every component.
  • The supply voltage is shared between components.
  • Total resistance is the sum of individual resistances.
\[ R_T=R_1+R_2+R_3+\cdots \]
Two resistors of 3 Ω and 5 Ω are connected in series. Find the total resistance.
\[ R_T=3+5 \] \[ \boxed{R_T=8\Omega} \]

12. Parallel Circuits

In a parallel circuit, components are connected across the same two points.

Important properties:

  • The potential difference across each parallel branch is the same.
  • The total current is the sum of branch currents.
  • The total resistance is less than the resistance of the smallest individual branch.
\[ \frac{1}{R_T} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} \]
Two resistors of 6 Ω and 3 Ω are connected in parallel. Find the total resistance.
\[ \frac1{R_T}=\frac16+\frac13 \] \[ \frac1{R_T}=\frac16+\frac26=\frac36=\frac12 \] \[ \boxed{R_T=2\Omega} \]

13. Electrical Power

Electrical power is the rate at which electrical energy is transferred.
\[ P=VI \]

Other useful forms are:

\[ P=I^2R \qquad P=\frac{V^2}{R} \]

Power is measured in watts (W).

A lamp operates at 12 V and draws a current of 2 A. Calculate its power.
\[ P=VI \] \[ P=12\times2 \] \[ \boxed{P=24\text{ W}} \]

14. Electrical Energy

\[ E=Pt \]

Electrical energy is measured in joules when power is in watts and time is in seconds.

A 100 W lamp operates for 60 s. Calculate the electrical energy transferred.
\[ E=Pt \] \[ E=100\times60 \] \[ \boxed{E=6000\text{ J}} \]

15. Electrical Energy in Kilowatt-hours

Electrical energy used by households is commonly measured in kilowatt-hours (kWh).

\[ \text{Energy (kWh)}=\text{Power (kW)}\times\text{time (h)} \]
A 2 kW heater operates for 3 hours. Find the energy consumed.
\[ E=2\times3 \] \[ \boxed{E=6\text{ kWh}} \]

16. Electrical Safety

Electricity can cause electric shock, burns and fires. Important safety devices and practices include:

  • fuses;
  • circuit breakers;
  • earthing;
  • insulation;
  • avoiding overloaded sockets;
  • keeping electrical equipment away from water;
  • switching off power before working on electrical equipment.
Never work on a live electrical circuit unless you are properly trained and authorised to do so.

17. Fuses and Circuit Breakers

A fuse contains a wire designed to melt when excessive current flows. This breaks the circuit and protects equipment.

A circuit breaker automatically opens the circuit when the current becomes dangerously high and can generally be reset after the fault is removed.

19. MANEB-Style Practice Questions

1. Define electric current.
2. A charge of 120 C passes through a wire in 30 s. Calculate the current.
3. A 24 V supply is connected to a 6 Ω resistor. Calculate the current.
4. State how an ammeter and a voltmeter are connected in a circuit.
5. Calculate the total resistance of 4 Ω, 6 Ω and 10 Ω resistors connected in series.
6. Calculate the total resistance of 6 Ω and 3 Ω resistors connected in parallel.
7. A 240 V appliance draws 5 A. Calculate its power.
8. A 1.5 kW heater operates for 4 hours. Calculate the energy consumed in kWh.

20. Answers

  1. The rate of flow of electric charge.
  2. \[ I=\frac{120}{30}=\boxed{4\text{ A}} \]
  3. \[ I=\frac{24}{6}=\boxed{4\text{ A}} \]
  4. Ammeter in series; voltmeter in parallel.
  5. \[ R_T=4+6+10=\boxed{20\Omega} \]
  6. \[ \frac1{R_T}=\frac16+\frac13=\frac12 \] \[ \boxed{R_T=2\Omega} \]
  7. \[ P=VI=240\times5=\boxed{1200\text{ W}} \]
  8. \[ E=Pt=1.5\times4=\boxed{6\text{ kWh}} \]

21. Common Mistakes

  • Connecting an ammeter in parallel.
  • Connecting a voltmeter in series.
  • Confusing current with voltage.
  • Using watts instead of kilowatts in kWh calculations.
  • Forgetting to convert time into hours when calculating kWh.
  • Using the series resistance formula for parallel circuits.

22. Examination Tips

  • Write the formula before substituting values.
  • Always include units.
  • Remember: current is measured in amperes, voltage in volts and resistance in ohms.
  • For circuit diagrams, use standard symbols and clear labels.
  • Check whether components are connected in series or parallel before choosing a resistance formula.
