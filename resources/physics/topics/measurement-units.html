Measurement and Units

Physics • Secondary School

1. Introduction

Physics depends on measurements. Scientists measure quantities such as length, mass, time, temperature and electric current so that observations can be described accurately and compared.

Physical quantity: A quantity that can be measured and expressed using a numerical value and a unit.

Learning objectives

  • Identify common physical quantities and their SI units.
  • Use measuring instruments correctly.
  • Convert between common units.
  • Distinguish scalar and vector quantities.
  • Record measurements with appropriate precision.

4. Measuring Length

Common instruments include:

  • ruler or metre rule
  • measuring tape
  • vernier calipers
  • micrometer screw gauge

Reading a ruler

Place one end of the object at the zero mark. Read the scale at the other end while viewing it directly to avoid parallax error.

Illustration of a ruler scale.

5. Measuring Mass

Mass can be measured using a beam balance or electronic balance.

Example: An object has a mass of 250 g. Express this in kilograms.

1000 g = 1 kg
250 g = 250 ÷ 1000 = 0.250 kg

6. Measuring Time

Time is measured using clocks, stopwatches and electronic timers.

Example: Convert 3 minutes to seconds.

3 × 60 = 180 s

7. Measuring Temperature

Temperature is commonly measured using a thermometer. The SI unit is the kelvin (K), although degrees Celsius (°C) are commonly used in school experiments.

\(T(K)=T(°C)+273\)
Example: Convert 27 °C to kelvin.

\(27+273=\mathbf{300\,K}\)

8. Scalars and Vectors

Scalar quantity: A physical quantity with magnitude only.

Examples: mass, time, temperature, distance, speed and energy.

Vector quantity: A physical quantity with magnitude and direction.

Examples: displacement, velocity, acceleration, force and weight.

9. Accuracy, Precision and Errors

Accuracy describes how close a measurement is to the true value.

Precision describes how closely repeated measurements agree with one another.

Parallax error occurs when a scale is viewed from an angle rather than directly above the reading.

Examination tip: When reading an analogue scale, place your eye directly in line with the scale marking to reduce parallax error.

11. MANEB-Style Examination Practice

  1. State the SI unit of mass.
  2. Distinguish between a scalar and a vector quantity.
  3. Convert 4.5 km into metres.
  4. Convert 750 g into kilograms.
  5. Convert 2 hours into seconds.
  6. Give two instruments used to measure length.
  7. Explain parallax error.
  8. Classify speed, velocity, force and mass as scalar or vector quantities.
  9. Convert 2500 cm³ into litres.
  10. State two ways of improving the reliability of an experiment.

Answers

  1. Kilogram (kg).
  2. A scalar has magnitude only; a vector has magnitude and direction.
  3. 4500 m.
  4. 0.75 kg.
  5. 7200 s.
  6. Ruler/metre rule, measuring tape, vernier calipers or micrometer.
  7. An error caused by viewing a scale from an incorrect angle.
  8. Speed—scalar; velocity—vector; force—vector; mass—scalar.
  9. 2.5 L.
  10. Repeat measurements and calculate an average; use suitable accurate apparatus.

12. Common Mistakes

  • Leaving units out of answers.
  • Confusing mass with weight.
  • Confusing distance with displacement.
  • Reading measuring instruments from an angle.
  • Using centimetres when the calculation requires metres.
  • Forgetting to convert time into seconds when using SI equations.

13. Examination Tips

  • Know the SI units and symbols.
  • Show unit conversions clearly.
  • Write the formula before substituting values.
  • Include units in final answers.
  • Use appropriate significant figures where required.
  • Label diagrams clearly.

Topic Summary

Measurement is the foundation of experimental physics. A good physics student must be able to identify quantities, use correct SI units, read instruments, convert units and recognise measurement errors.
