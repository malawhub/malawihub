Solar Energy and Renewable Energy

Physics • Secondary School • MANEB-focused study notes

1. Introduction

Energy is required for lighting, heating, transport, communication, agriculture and industry. Renewable energy sources can be naturally replenished and can reduce dependence on finite fossil fuels.

2. Learning Objectives

  • Define renewable and non-renewable energy.
  • Explain solar energy.
  • Describe solar photovoltaic cells.
  • Explain solar heating.
  • Describe wind, hydroelectric, biomass and geothermal energy.
  • Compare advantages and disadvantages of renewable energy sources.
  • Explain energy efficiency and conservation.
  • Apply renewable-energy ideas to everyday situations in Malawi.

3. Renewable and Non-Renewable Energy

Renewable energy comes from sources that are naturally replenished over a relatively short period.
Non-renewable energy comes from finite resources that take very long periods to form.

4. Solar Energy

Solar energy is energy received from the Sun in the form of electromagnetic radiation.

Solar radiation can be used to produce electricity and heat.

5. Solar Photovoltaic Cells

Photovoltaic (PV) cell is a device that converts light energy directly into electrical energy.

Several PV cells can be connected together to form a solar panel.

The energy conversion is:

Solar radiation → electrical energy

Solar panels can supply electricity for lighting, charging batteries, powering pumps and operating electrical equipment.

6. Factors Affecting Solar Panel Output

The electrical output of a solar panel depends on factors such as:

  • intensity of sunlight;
  • area of the panel;
  • orientation of the panel;
  • shading;
  • temperature and panel characteristics;
  • condition of the equipment.
Shade covering part of a solar panel can reduce its electrical output.

7. Solar Heating

Solar energy can also be used directly for heating.

Examples include:

  • solar water heaters;
  • solar cookers;
  • solar dryers.

Dark surfaces generally absorb more radiant energy than shiny light-coloured surfaces.

8. Solar Water Heating

A solar water-heating system uses solar radiation to raise the temperature of water.

  1. Solar radiation is absorbed by a collector.
  2. The collector becomes warm.
  3. Heat is transferred to water.
  4. Warm water is stored or supplied for use.

9. Hydroelectric Energy

Hydroelectric energy is electrical energy generated using moving or falling water.

Water stored at a height has gravitational potential energy. As it flows downward, this energy is converted into kinetic energy, which turns a turbine connected to a generator.

Energy conversion:
Gravitational potential energy → kinetic energy → mechanical energy → electrical energy

10. Wind Energy

Wind energy is energy obtained from moving air.

Wind turns turbine blades. The rotating turbine can drive a generator to produce electricity.

Energy conversion:
Kinetic energy of wind → mechanical energy → electrical energy

11. Biomass Energy

Biomass is organic material from plants and animals that can be used as an energy source.

Examples include:

  • wood;
  • crop residues;
  • animal waste;
  • biogas;
  • other organic materials.

Biomass can be burned directly or converted into fuels such as biogas.

12. Biogas

Biogas is produced when microorganisms break down organic matter in the absence of oxygen.

Biogas can be used for:

  • cooking;
  • heating;
  • some forms of electricity generation.

A major component of biogas is methane.

13. Geothermal Energy

Geothermal energy is energy obtained from heat inside the Earth.

Where suitable geological conditions exist, underground heat can be used for heating or electricity generation.

14. Tidal and Wave Energy

Ocean tides and waves contain mechanical energy that can be converted into electrical energy using suitable technology.

These sources are renewable but are more practical in locations with suitable coastal conditions.

15. Advantages of Renewable Energy

  • Sources are naturally replenished.
  • Solar and wind energy do not require fuel combustion during electricity generation.
  • Can reduce dependence on imported fuels.
  • Can support electricity access in remote areas.
  • Can reduce some forms of air pollution.
  • Solar systems can be useful where grid electricity is unavailable.

16. Disadvantages and Limitations

  • Solar power depends on available sunlight.
  • Wind power depends on wind conditions.
  • Hydroelectric generation can depend on water availability.
  • Some systems have high initial installation costs.
  • Energy storage may be needed for variable sources.
  • Large projects can affect land, ecosystems or communities.

17. Energy Efficiency

Energy efficiency describes how effectively an energy system converts input energy into useful output.
\[ \text{Efficiency} = \frac{\text{useful energy output}} {\text{total energy input}} \times100\% \]
A device receives 500 J of energy and produces 350 J of useful output. Calculate its efficiency.
\[ \text{Efficiency} = \frac{350}{500}\times100\% \] \[ \boxed{70\%} \]

18. Energy Conservation

Energy conservation means reducing unnecessary energy use while maintaining the required service.

Examples include:

  • switching off unused lights;
  • using efficient lamps;
  • reducing unnecessary heating;
  • maintaining electrical equipment;
  • using appropriately sized appliances.

19. Renewable Energy in Malawi

Renewable energy can be particularly useful for communities that are far from electricity distribution networks.

Possible applications include:

  • solar home systems;
  • solar lighting;
  • solar-powered water pumping;
  • solar charging systems;
  • small-scale hydroelectric systems;
  • biogas for cooking.
The most suitable energy source depends on local conditions, cost, reliability, environmental effects and the energy requirement.

21. MANEB-Style Practice Questions

1. Define renewable energy.
2. State four renewable energy sources.
3. Explain how a solar photovoltaic cell produces electricity.
4. State three factors that can affect the output of a solar panel.
5. Describe the energy transformations in a hydroelectric power station.
6. Give two advantages and two limitations of solar energy.
7. A machine receives 800 J of energy and produces 600 J of useful energy. Calculate its efficiency.
8. What is biomass?
9. State two uses of biogas.
10. Explain why solar energy can be useful in rural areas without reliable grid electricity.

22. Answers

  1. Energy obtained from a source that is naturally replenished.
  2. Solar, wind, hydroelectric, biomass and geothermal energy.
  3. Light energy causes electrical charges in the photovoltaic material to move, producing an electrical current.
  4. Sunlight intensity, panel area, shading, orientation and equipment condition.
  5. Gravitational potential energy → kinetic energy → mechanical energy → electrical energy.
  6. Advantages include renewable supply and low operating emissions; limitations include dependence on sunlight and initial installation cost.
  7. \[ \text{Efficiency} = \frac{600}{800}\times100\% = \boxed{75\%} \]
  8. Organic material from plants or animals that can be used as an energy source.
  9. Cooking and heating; it can also be used for electricity generation.
  10. Solar systems can generate electricity independently of the electricity grid, making them useful for lighting, charging and other basic electrical needs where grid access is limited.

23. Common Mistakes

  • Calling fossil fuels renewable.
  • Confusing solar photovoltaic cells with solar water heaters.
  • Forgetting the energy transformations in hydroelectric systems.
  • Using watts instead of kilowatts without conversion when required.
  • Confusing energy efficiency with energy conservation.
  • Assuming renewable energy sources have no environmental impact.

24. Examination Tips

  • When asked about advantages, give clear points and explain them.
  • Always show energy transformations using arrows.
  • For efficiency calculations, write the formula first.
  • Use local examples where appropriate.
  • Remember that renewable does not necessarily mean completely pollution-free.
