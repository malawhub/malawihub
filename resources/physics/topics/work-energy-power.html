Work, Energy and Power

Physics • Secondary School

1. Introduction

Work, energy and power are closely related ideas used to describe how forces transfer energy and how quickly energy is transferred.

Learning objectives

  • Define work, energy and power.
  • Calculate work done by a force.
  • Identify common forms of energy.
  • Apply conservation of energy.
  • Calculate power and efficiency.

2. Work Done

Work is done when a force causes an object to move through a distance in the direction of the force.
\[ W=Fd \]

where \(W\) is work done in joules (J), \(F\) is force in newtons (N), and \(d\) is distance moved in metres (m).

Example: A force of 20 N moves a box through 5 m.

\[ W=Fd=20\times5=\mathbf{100\ J} \]
If there is no movement in the direction of the applied force, no mechanical work is done by that force.

3. Energy

Energy is the capacity to do work. Its SI unit is the joule (J).

Common forms of energy

FormExample
KineticA moving car
Gravitational potentialAn object held above the ground
Elastic potentialA stretched spring
ChemicalFuel or food
ThermalHot water

LightEnergy from the Sun
SoundEnergy produced by vibrating objects

4. Kinetic Energy

Kinetic energy is the energy possessed by an object because of its motion.
\[ E_k=\frac{1}{2}mv^2 \]
A 4 kg object moves at 5 m/s.

\[ E_k=\frac12(4)(5^2) \] \[ E_k=2\times25=\mathbf{50\ J} \]
Because velocity is squared, increasing the speed can greatly increase the kinetic energy.

5. Gravitational Potential Energy

Gravitational potential energy is energy stored because of an object's position in a gravitational field.
\[ E_p=mgh \]
A 2 kg object is raised through 5 m. Take \(g=10\ m/s^2\).

\[ E_p=2\times10\times5=\mathbf{100\ J} \]

6. Elastic Potential Energy

Elastic potential energy is stored in an elastic object when it is stretched or compressed.

\[ E_e=\frac12kx^2 \]

where \(k\) is the spring constant and \(x\) is the extension or compression.

7. Conservation of Energy

Energy cannot be created or destroyed. It can only be transferred from one form to another.
When a ball falls:

Gravitational potential energy → kinetic energy → thermal and sound energy.
In a real system, some useful energy is often transferred to the surroundings as thermal energy or sound because of friction and air resistance.

8. Power

Power is the rate at which work is done or energy is transferred.
\[ P=\frac{W}{t} \]

The SI unit of power is the watt (W).

A machine does 600 J of work in 20 s.

\[ P=\frac{600}{20}=\mathbf{30\ W} \]

Also:

\[ P=\frac{E}{t} \]

9. Efficiency

Efficiency compares useful energy output with total energy input.
\[ \text{Efficiency}= \frac{\text{useful energy output}} {\text{total energy input}}\times100\% \]
A machine receives 500 J and produces 400 J of useful energy.

\[ \text{Efficiency}= \frac{400}{500}\times100 =\mathbf{80\%} \]
No real machine is 100% efficient because some energy is dissipated to the surroundings, commonly as heat and sound.

10. Mechanical Advantage and Energy

Machines make tasks easier by changing the size or direction of a force. Examples include levers, pulleys, gears and inclined planes.

\[ MA=\frac{\text{load}}{\text{effort}} \]

Mechanical advantage has no unit.

If a machine lifts a 600 N load using an effort of 200 N:

\[ MA=\frac{600}{200}=\mathbf{3} \]

11. Work and Force-Distance Graphs

When a constant force acts in the direction of motion, work done is equal to the area under a force-distance graph.

For a constant force:

\[ W=Fd \]

Therefore, a larger force or a larger distance produces more work.

12. MANEB-Style Examination Practice

  1. Define work and state its SI unit.
  2. A force of 50 N moves an object 4 m. Calculate the work done.
  3. Define kinetic energy.
  4. Calculate the kinetic energy of a 10 kg object moving at 6 m/s.
  5. A 5 kg object is raised through 8 m. Take \(g=10\ m/s^2\). Calculate its gravitational potential energy.
  6. Define power and state its SI unit.
  7. A motor transfers 3000 J of energy in 60 s. Calculate its power.
  8. A machine receives 1000 J and gives 700 J useful output. Calculate its efficiency.
  9. State the law of conservation of energy.
  10. Explain why a real machine cannot normally be 100% efficient.

Answers

  1. Work is done when a force causes displacement in the direction of the force; joule (J).
  2. \(200\ J\).
  3. Energy possessed because of motion.
  4. \(180\ J\).
  5. \(400\ J\).
  6. Rate of doing work or transferring energy; watt (W).
  7. \(50\ W\).
  8. \(70\%\).
  9. Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred or transformed.
  10. Some energy is dissipated to the surroundings, especially as heat and sound.

13. Common Mistakes

  • Using centimetres instead of metres in work calculations.
  • Forgetting to square velocity in kinetic-energy calculations.
  • Confusing energy with power.
  • Giving power in joules instead of watts.
  • Forgetting to multiply efficiency by 100%.
  • Using mass in grams instead of kilograms.

14. Examination Tips

  • Write the formula before substitution.
  • Convert quantities to appropriate SI units.
  • Show every calculation step.
  • Include units in final answers.
  • When explaining energy transfers, name both the initial and final forms.
  • For efficiency questions, identify useful output and total input carefully.

Topic Summary

Work describes energy transfer by forces. Energy exists in different forms, including kinetic and potential energy. Power describes how quickly work is done, while efficiency describes how much of the input energy becomes useful output.
