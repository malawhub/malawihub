Density and Pressure

Physics • Secondary School

1. Introduction

Density describes how much mass is contained in a given volume. Pressure describes how concentrated a force is over an area. These ideas are important in solids, liquids, gases and everyday applications.

Learning objectives

  • Define density and calculate it.
  • Determine the density of regular and irregular objects.
  • Explain pressure in solids.
  • Calculate pressure using force and area.
  • Explain pressure in liquids and gases.
  • Apply pressure principles to everyday situations.

2. Density

Density is the mass per unit volume of a substance.
\[ \rho=\frac{m}{V} \]

where:

  • \(\rho\) = density
  • \(m\) = mass
  • \(V\) = volume

The SI unit of density is kg/m³.

Example: An object has a mass of 600 kg and a volume of 3 m³. Find its density.

\[ \rho=\frac{600}{3}=\mathbf{200\ kg/m^3} \]

3. Density Triangle

The density relationship can be rearranged:

\[ \rho=\frac{m}{V} \qquad m=\rho V \qquad V=\frac{m}{\rho} \]
Before calculating, check that the units of mass and volume are compatible with the required density unit.
A metal has density \(8000\ kg/m^3\) and volume \(0.002\ m^3\). Find its mass.

\[ m=\rho V \] \[ m=8000\times0.002 =\mathbf{16\ kg} \]

4. Measuring the Density of a Regular Solid

For a regular object, measure its dimensions and calculate its volume.

For a rectangular block:

\[ V=lwh \]

Then calculate:

\[ \rho=\frac{m}{lwh} \]
A rectangular block has length 10 cm, width 5 cm and height 2 cm. Its mass is 300 g.

\[ V=10\times5\times2=100\ cm^3 \] \[ \rho=\frac{300}{100} =\mathbf{3\ g/cm^3} \]

5. Measuring the Density of an Irregular Solid

The volume of an irregular solid can be determined using the displacement method.

  1. Put water into a measuring cylinder.
  2. Record the initial volume.
  3. Carefully immerse the object completely.
  4. Record the final volume.
  5. Subtract the initial volume from the final volume.
\[ V_{\text{object}}=V_{\text{final}}-V_{\text{initial}} \]
The initial water volume is 40 cm³ and the final volume is 65 cm³.

\[ V=65-40=\mathbf{25\ cm^3} \]
Remove air bubbles and ensure the object is completely submerged when using water displacement.

6. Relative Density

Relative density is the ratio of the density of a substance to the density of water.
\[ \text{Relative density}= \frac{\text{density of substance}} {\text{density of water}} \]

Relative density has no unit.

The density of water is approximately \(1000\ kg/m^3\) or \(1\ g/cm^3\).
A liquid has density \(800\ kg/m^3\).

\[ RD=\frac{800}{1000}=\mathbf{0.8} \]

7. Floating and Sinking

An object floats when the upward buoyant force can support its weight. Whether an object tends to float or sink is also related to its density.

  • If the object's average density is less than the liquid, it tends to float.
  • If its density is greater than the liquid, it tends to sink.
Wood generally floats on water because its average density is less than the density of water.

8. Pressure

Pressure is the force acting normally per unit area.
\[ P=\frac{F}{A} \]

The SI unit of pressure is the pascal (Pa).

\(1\ Pa=1\ N/m^2\)

A force of 200 N acts on an area of 4 m².

\[ P=\frac{200}{4} =\mathbf{50\ Pa} \]

9. Pressure in Solids

For the same force, reducing the area increases pressure. Increasing the area reduces pressure.

A sharp knife cuts more easily than a blunt knife because its sharp edge has a much smaller contact area, producing greater pressure for the same applied force.
SituationEffect
Small areaHigh pressure
Large areaLow pressure

This explains why wide foundations are used for buildings and why snowshoes have large surface areas.

10. Pressure in Liquids

Liquid pressure increases with depth because deeper points have a greater column of liquid above them.

\[ P=\rho gh \]

where:

  • \(\rho\) = density of liquid
  • \(g\) = gravitational acceleration
  • \(h\) = depth
Water has density \(1000\ kg/m^3\). Calculate the pressure due to water at a depth of 2 m, taking \(g=10\ m/s^2\).

\[ P=1000\times10\times2 =\mathbf{20000\ Pa} \]
Liquid pressure depends on density, gravitational field strength and depth. It does not directly depend on the shape of the container.

11. Atmospheric Pressure

The Earth's atmosphere has mass and therefore exerts pressure on surfaces. Atmospheric pressure acts in all directions.

Atmospheric pressure generally decreases as altitude increases because there is less air above the observer.

Drinking through a straw works because atmospheric pressure acting on the liquid surface helps push the liquid up the straw when pressure inside the straw is reduced.

12. Hydraulic Systems

Liquids are difficult to compress, so pressure applied to an enclosed liquid can be transmitted through the liquid.

\[ P=\frac{F}{A} \]

Hydraulic brakes, jacks and lifts use this principle.

If a force of 100 N acts on an area of \(0.01\ m^2\):

\[ P=\frac{100}{0.01} =\mathbf{10000\ Pa} \]

13. MANEB-Style Examination Practice

  1. Define density and state its SI unit.
  2. A substance has a mass of 500 g and a volume of 250 cm³. Calculate its density.
  3. An object has a density of \(2500\ kg/m^3\) and a volume of \(0.004\ m^3\). Calculate its mass.
  4. Explain how the volume of an irregular solid can be measured.
  5. Define pressure and state its SI unit.
  6. A force of 600 N acts on an area of 3 m². Calculate the pressure.
  7. Explain why a sharp nail produces greater pressure than a blunt nail for the same force.
  8. Calculate the pressure due to water at a depth of 5 m. Take \(\rho=1000\ kg/m^3\) and \(g=10\ m/s^2\).
  9. State two factors that affect liquid pressure.
  10. Explain why atmospheric pressure decreases with altitude.

Answers

  1. Mass per unit volume; kg/m³.
  2. \(2\ g/cm^3\).
  3. \(10\ kg\).
  4. Use water displacement: final volume minus initial volume.
  5. Force per unit area; pascal (Pa).
  6. \(200\ Pa\).
  7. The nail has a smaller contact area, so the same force produces greater pressure.
  8. \(50000\ Pa\).
  9. Depth and liquid density; gravitational field strength also affects it.
  10. There is less air above at greater altitude, so the weight of the air column is smaller.

14. Common Mistakes

  • Using mass instead of force in the pressure formula.
  • Forgetting to convert area into m².
  • Confusing density with relative density.
  • Forgetting that relative density has no unit.
  • Using the wrong volume when applying water displacement.
  • Thinking liquid pressure is the same at every depth.

15. Examination Tips

  • Write the formula before substitution.
  • Use SI units where required.
  • For displacement experiments, clearly identify initial and final readings.
  • Remember that pressure increases when area decreases for a fixed force.
  • For liquid-pressure calculations, check all three quantities in \(P=\rho gh\).
  • Always include the correct unit in numerical answers.

Topic Summary

Density relates mass to volume, while pressure relates force to area. Density measurements use mass and volume, and pressure in liquids increases with depth. These principles explain many practical devices and everyday observations.
