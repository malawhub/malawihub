Motion, Speed and Acceleration

Physics • Secondary School

1. Introduction

Motion occurs when an object changes its position with time. Physics describes motion using quantities such as distance, displacement, speed, velocity and acceleration.

Learning objectives

  • Define distance and displacement.
  • Calculate speed and velocity.
  • Calculate acceleration.
  • Use motion equations correctly.
  • Interpret distance-time and velocity-time graphs.

2. Distance and Displacement

Distance is the total length of the path travelled by an object. It is a scalar quantity.
Displacement is the straight-line distance from the starting position to the final position in a specified direction. It is a vector quantity.
Example: A learner walks 300 m east and then 100 m west.

Distance = 300 + 100 = 400 m
Displacement = 300 − 100 = 200 m east

3. Speed

Speed is the distance travelled per unit time.
\[ v=\frac{d}{t} \]

where \(v\) is speed, \(d\) is distance and \(t\) is time.

Worked example: A car travels 150 m in 10 s. Calculate its speed.

\[ v=\frac{150}{10}=\mathbf{15\ m/s} \]

Common units

  • m/s — SI unit
  • km/h — commonly used for vehicles
To convert km/h to m/s, multiply by \(\frac{5}{18}\).
To convert m/s to km/h, multiply by \(\frac{18}{5}\).
Example: Convert 72 km/h to m/s.

\[ 72\times\frac{5}{18}=\mathbf{20\ m/s} \]

4. Velocity

Velocity is the rate of change of displacement with time.
\[ v=\frac{\text{displacement}}{\text{time}} \]

Velocity has both magnitude and direction.

A cyclist moves 120 m north in 20 s.

\[ v=\frac{120}{20}=\mathbf{6\ m/s\ north} \]

5. Acceleration

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity with time.
\[ a=\frac{v-u}{t} \]

where:

  • \(a\) = acceleration
  • \(v\) = final velocity
  • \(u\) = initial velocity
  • \(t\) = time
Worked example: A car increases its velocity from 5 m/s to 25 m/s in 4 s.

\[ a=\frac{25-5}{4} =\frac{20}{4} =\mathbf{5\ m/s^2} \]
Negative acceleration is often called deceleration or retardation. It means that velocity is decreasing in the chosen direction.

6. Uniform and Non-uniform Motion

Uniform motion: An object travels equal distances in equal time intervals.

Non-uniform motion: The object's speed or velocity changes with time.

MotionTypical description
UniformConstant speed
AcceleratingSpeed increasing
DeceleratingSpeed decreasing
StationaryPosition does not change

7. Distance-Time Graphs

A distance-time graph shows how distance changes with time.

The gradient of a distance-time graph represents speed.

Steeper gradient = greater speed. A horizontal line means the object is stationary.

8. Velocity-Time Graphs

A velocity-time graph shows how velocity changes with time.

The gradient of a velocity-time graph represents acceleration.

The area under a velocity-time graph represents displacement.

Graph featureMeaning
Horizontal lineConstant velocity
Positive slopePositive acceleration
Negative slopeDeceleration
Area under graphDisplacement

9. Equations of Uniformly Accelerated Motion

For motion with constant acceleration, the following equations are useful:

\[ v=u+at \]
\[ s=ut+\frac{1}{2}at^2 \]
\[ v^2=u^2+2as \]

where \(s\) is displacement.

Example: A vehicle starts from rest and accelerates at \(2\ m/s^2\) for 5 s. Find its final velocity.

Given \(u=0,\ a=2,\ t=5\)
\[ v=u+at \] \[ v=0+(2)(5)=\mathbf{10\ m/s} \]

10. Average Speed

\[ \text{Average speed}= \frac{\text{total distance}}{\text{total time}} \]
A car travels 100 km in 2 h and then 50 km in 1 h.

Total distance = 150 km
Total time = 3 h
Average speed = \[ \frac{150}{3}=\mathbf{50\ km/h} \]
Do not calculate average speed by simply averaging two speeds unless the time intervals are equal.

11. MANEB-Style Practice

  1. Define distance and displacement.
  2. A bus travels 240 m in 20 s. Calculate its speed.
  3. Convert 90 km/h into m/s.
  4. A runner changes velocity from 4 m/s to 12 m/s in 4 s. Find the acceleration.
  5. Explain the difference between speed and velocity.
  6. What does a horizontal line on a distance-time graph represent?
  7. What physical quantity is represented by the gradient of a velocity-time graph?
  8. A car starts from rest and accelerates at \(3\ m/s^2\) for 6 s. Find its final velocity.
  9. State two differences between uniform and non-uniform motion.
  10. Explain what the area under a velocity-time graph represents.

Answers

  1. Distance is total path length; displacement is distance in a specified direction from start to finish.
  2. \(12\ m/s\).
  3. \(25\ m/s\).
  4. \(2\ m/s^2\).
  5. Speed is scalar; velocity is vector and includes direction.
  6. The object is stationary.
  7. Acceleration.
  8. \(18\ m/s\).
  9. Uniform motion has constant velocity; non-uniform motion has changing velocity.
  10. Displacement.

12. Common Mistakes

  • Confusing distance with displacement.
  • Confusing speed with velocity.
  • Forgetting to convert km/h into m/s.
  • Using \(u\) and \(v\) incorrectly in equations.
  • Giving acceleration in m/s instead of m/s².
  • Thinking the gradient of a distance-time graph is acceleration.

13. Examination Tips

  • Write down all known quantities before calculating.
  • Choose the equation containing the quantities given in the question.
  • Show substitution and working.
  • Give the correct unit in the final answer.
  • For graphs, label both axes and include units.
  • Pay attention to direction when dealing with vectors.

Topic Summary

Motion is described using distance, displacement, speed, velocity and acceleration. Graphs and equations allow motion to be analysed quantitatively. These ideas form an important foundation for mechanics and examination questions.
