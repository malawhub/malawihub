1. Introduction

Statistics is the branch of mathematics concerned with collecting, organising, presenting, analysing and interpreting data.

Learning objectives

  • Understand different types of data.
  • Organise data in tables.
  • Calculate mean, median and mode.
  • Use frequency tables.
  • Calculate range and understand measures of spread.
  • Interpret statistical diagrams.
  • Construct and interpret bar charts, histograms and pie charts.
  • Use cumulative frequency.
  • Answer examination questions involving statistics.

2. Data

Data means information collected for analysis.

Primary data

Information collected directly by the researcher, for example through questionnaires, interviews or measurements.

Secondary data

Information obtained from an existing source such as a report, book, website or government publication.

Discrete data

Data that can be counted and normally takes separate values. For example, the number of students in a class.

Continuous data

Data that can take any value within a range, such as height, mass or time.

4. Mean

The mean is obtained by adding all values and dividing by the number of values.

\[ \text{Mean}= \frac{\text{sum of values}}{\text{number of values}} \]
Find the mean of: \[ 4,\ 6,\ 8,\ 10,\ 12 \] \[ \text{Mean}= \frac{4+6+8+10+12}{5} \] \[ =\frac{40}{5} \] \[ \boxed{8} \]

Mean from a frequency table

\[ \bar{x}=\frac{\sum fx}{\sum f} \]

5. Median

The median is the middle value when the data is arranged in order.

Find the median of: \[ 3,\ 7,\ 2,\ 9,\ 5 \] Arrange in ascending order: \[ 2,\ 3,\ 5,\ 7,\ 9 \] The middle value is: \[ \boxed{5} \]
If there is an even number of observations, the median is the mean of the two middle values.
Find the median of: \[ 2,\ 4,\ 6,\ 8 \] The two middle values are 4 and 6. \[ \text{Median}=\frac{4+6}{2} \] \[ \boxed{5} \]

6. Mode

The mode is the value that occurs most frequently.

Find the mode: \[ 2,\ 4,\ 4,\ 5,\ 6,\ 4,\ 7 \] The number 4 occurs three times. \[ \boxed{\text{Mode}=4} \]

7. Range

The range measures the difference between the largest and smallest values.

\[ \text{Range}=\text{largest value}-\text{smallest value} \]
For: \[ 5,\ 8,\ 12,\ 14,\ 20 \] \[ \text{Range}=20-5 \] \[ \boxed{15} \]

8. Bar Charts

A bar chart is useful for displaying discrete or categorical data. Bars are separated by spaces.

9. Pie Charts

A pie chart represents data as sectors of a circle.

\[ \text{Sector angle} = \frac{\text{frequency}}{\text{total frequency}} \times360^\circ \]

10. Histograms

A histogram is used for continuous grouped data. Unlike a bar chart, the bars touch each other.

For unequal class intervals, frequency density is used.
\[ \text{Frequency density} = \frac{\text{frequency}}{\text{class width}} \]

12. Grouped Data

When data contains many observations, values can be grouped into class intervals.

For grouped data, the midpoint of each class is often used to estimate the mean.

\[ \text{Estimated mean} = \frac{\sum fx}{\sum f} \]
The midpoint of a class interval is: \[ \frac{\text{lower boundary}+\text{upper boundary}}{2} \]

13. MANEB-style Practice

  1. Find the mean of \(6,8,10,12,14\).
  2. Find the median of \(3,9,5,7,11\).
  3. Find the mode of \(2,3,3,4,5,3,6\).
  4. Find the range of \(8,12,15,21,25\).
  5. A class has 40 students. 16 prefer football. Find the angle representing football on a pie chart.
  6. Explain one difference between a bar chart and a histogram.
  7. Calculate the frequency density for a class of width 5 having frequency 20.
  8. Find the cumulative frequencies for frequencies \(3,7,4,6\).

Answers

  1. \(10\)
  2. \(7\)
  3. \(3\)
  4. \(17\)
  5. \[ \frac{16}{40}\times360^\circ=\boxed{144^\circ} \]
  6. Bars in a bar chart are separated; histogram bars touch.
  7. \[ \frac{20}{5}=\boxed{4} \]
  8. \(3,10,14,20\)

14. Common Mistakes

  • Forgetting to arrange values before finding the median.
  • Confusing the mean with the median.
  • Using the wrong total frequency in pie-chart calculations.
  • Leaving gaps between histogram bars.
  • Forgetting class width when calculating frequency density.
  • Adding frequencies incorrectly when constructing cumulative frequency.
Examination tip: Always show your working. When interpreting a graph or table, quote the values you used before giving your conclusion.
