1. Introduction to Geometry

Geometry is the study of shapes, sizes, positions and relationships between figures. Mensuration is concerned with measuring lengths, areas, surface areas and volumes.

Learning objectives

  • Use basic angle properties.
  • Work with triangles and quadrilaterals.
  • Apply Pythagoras' theorem.
  • Calculate perimeter and area.
  • Calculate surface area and volume.
  • Apply circle geometry.
  • Solve practical measurement problems.

2. Basic Angle Facts

  • Angles on a straight line add up to \(180^\circ\).
  • Angles around a point add up to \(360^\circ\).
  • Vertically opposite angles are equal.
  • Angles in a triangle add up to \(180^\circ\).
  • Angles in a quadrilateral add up to \(360^\circ\).
Two angles on a straight line are \(65^\circ\) and \(x\). \[ x+65^\circ=180^\circ \] Therefore: \[ \boxed{x=115^\circ} \]

3. Parallel Lines

When a transversal crosses two parallel lines, several important angle relationships are formed.

  • Corresponding angles are equal.
  • Alternate angles are equal.
  • Co-interior angles add up to \(180^\circ\).

4. Triangles

Triangle Property
Equilateral Three equal sides and three \(60^\circ\) angles
Isosceles Two equal sides and two equal base angles
Scalene All sides are different
Right-angled One angle is \(90^\circ\)
An isosceles triangle has two equal angles of \(50^\circ\). The third angle is: \[ 180^\circ-50^\circ-50^\circ \] \[ \boxed{80^\circ} \]

5. Pythagoras' Theorem

Pythagoras' theorem applies to right-angled triangles.

\[ a^2+b^2=c^2 \]

Here \(c\) is the hypotenuse, which is the side opposite the right angle.

A right triangle has shorter sides of 6 cm and 8 cm. \[ c^2=6^2+8^2 \] \[ c^2=36+64=100 \] \[ c=\sqrt{100} \] \[ \boxed{c=10\text{ cm}} \]

6. Perimeter

The perimeter is the total distance around a two-dimensional shape.

Rectangle

\[ P=2(l+w) \]

Square

\[ P=4s \]

Circle

\[ C=2\pi r \]
A rectangle has length 12 cm and width 5 cm. \[ P=2(12+5) \] \[ \boxed{P=34\text{ cm}} \]

7. Area

Rectangle

\[ A=lw \]

Square

\[ A=s^2 \]

Triangle

\[ A=\frac12bh \]

Parallelogram

\[ A=bh \]

Trapezium

\[ A=\frac12(a+b)h \]

Circle

\[ A=\pi r^2 \]
Find the area of a triangle with base 14 cm and height 8 cm. \[ A=\frac12(14)(8) \] \[ \boxed{A=56\text{ cm}^2} \]

8. Circle Geometry

Important circle facts include:

  • The angle in a semicircle is \(90^\circ\).
  • Angles in the same segment are equal.
  • The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference standing on the same arc.
  • A radius is perpendicular to a tangent at the point of contact.
  • Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to \(180^\circ\).
If an angle at the circumference is \(35^\circ\), the angle at the centre standing on the same arc is: \[ 2(35^\circ) \] \[ \boxed{70^\circ} \]

9. Surface Area

Surface area is the total area of all the surfaces of a three-dimensional object.

Cuboid

\[ SA=2(lw+lh+wh) \]

Cube

\[ SA=6s^2 \]

Cylinder

\[ SA=2\pi r^2+2\pi rh \]

10. Volume

Cuboid

\[ V=lwh \]

Cube

\[ V=s^3 \]

Cylinder

\[ V=\pi r^2h \]

Sphere

\[ V=\frac43\pi r^3 \]
A cuboid measures 8 cm by 5 cm by 3 cm. \[ V=8(5)(3) \] \[ \boxed{V=120\text{ cm}^3} \]

11. Unit Conversions

Measurement Relationship
Length \(1\text{ m}=100\text{ cm}\)
Area \(1\text{ m}^2=10\,000\text{ cm}^2\)
Volume \(1\text{ m}^3=1\,000\,000\text{ cm}^3\)
Capacity \(1\text{ litre}=1000\text{ cm}^3\)
Important: When converting area or volume, the conversion factor must also be squared or cubed respectively.

12. MANEB-style Practice

  1. Find the third angle of a triangle whose other angles are \(65^\circ\) and \(45^\circ\).
  2. A right-angled triangle has sides 9 cm and 12 cm. Find its hypotenuse.
  3. Find the area of a rectangle measuring 15 cm by 8 cm.
  4. Find the area of a circle of radius 7 cm using \(\pi=\frac{22}{7}\).
  5. Find the volume of a cuboid measuring 6 cm by 4 cm by 5 cm.
  6. A square has a perimeter of 36 cm. Find its side length and area.
  7. Two angles in a triangle are \(40^\circ\) and \(75^\circ\). Find the third angle.
  8. Find the circumference of a circle with radius 14 cm using \(\pi=\frac{22}{7}\).

Answers

  1. \(70^\circ\)
  2. \(15\text{ cm}\)
  3. \(120\text{ cm}^2\)
  4. \(154\text{ cm}^2\)
  5. \(120\text{ cm}^3\)
  6. Side \(=9\text{ cm}\), area \(=81\text{ cm}^2\)
  7. \(65^\circ\)
  8. \(88\text{ cm}\)

13. Common Mistakes

  • Using Pythagoras on a triangle that is not right-angled.
  • Using diameter instead of radius in circle formulas.
  • Forgetting the \(\frac12\) in the area of a triangle.
  • Confusing perimeter with area.
  • Writing square units as ordinary units.
  • Writing cubic units incorrectly for volume.
  • Forgetting to convert units before calculating.
Examination tip: Write the formula first, substitute the values carefully, show the calculation and give the final answer with the correct unit.
