Sequences and Patterns

Mathematics • Secondary School

1. Introduction

A sequence is an ordered list of numbers that follows a particular rule or pattern. The numbers in a sequence are called terms.

Example:
\(2,\ 4,\ 6,\ 8,\ 10,\ldots\)
The terms increase by 2 each time.

Sequences are important because they help us recognise patterns, predict future values and develop algebraic rules.

2. Learning Objectives

By the end of this section, you should be able to:

  • identify patterns in sequences;
  • find missing terms;
  • describe the rule of a sequence;
  • find the next terms of a sequence;
  • work with arithmetic sequences;
  • find the nth term of simple sequences;
  • solve examination-style sequence problems.

3. Important Vocabulary

Term Meaning
Sequence An ordered list of numbers following a pattern.
Term One number in a sequence.
First term The first number in the sequence.
Common difference The amount added or subtracted between consecutive terms.
nth term A formula used to find any term in a sequence.

4. Recognising Patterns

To identify a sequence rule, compare consecutive terms.

Example 1
\(5,\ 8,\ 11,\ 14,\ 17,\ldots\)

Each term increases by 3.

\(+3,\ +3,\ +3,\ +3\)

Therefore, the rule is add 3.

Example 2
\(30,\ 25,\ 20,\ 15,\ 10,\ldots\)

Each term decreases by 5.

\(-5,\ -5,\ -5,\ -5\)

5. Finding Missing Terms

If the pattern is known, a missing term can be found by applying the same rule.

Example:
\(4,\ 7,\ \square,\ 13,\ 16\)

The difference is \(+3\).

\[ 7+3=10 \] Therefore, the missing term is \[ \boxed{10} \]

6. Arithmetic Sequences

An arithmetic sequence is a sequence in which the difference between consecutive terms is constant.

Common difference: \[ d=\text{second term}-\text{first term} \]

For example:

\(3,\ 7,\ 11,\ 15,\ 19,\ldots\)

The common difference is

\(d=7-3=4\)

7. The nth Term of an Arithmetic Sequence

The nth term of an arithmetic sequence is given by:

\[ T_n=a+(n-1)d \]

where:

  • \(T_n\) is the nth term;
  • \(a\) is the first term;
  • \(n\) is the position of the term;
  • \(d\) is the common difference.
Example:

Find the 20th term of:

\(5,\ 8,\ 11,\ 14,\ldots\)
First term: \[ a=5 \] Common difference: \[ d=3 \] Therefore: \[ T_{20}=5+(20-1)(3) \] \[ T_{20}=5+57 \] \[ \boxed{T_{20}=62} \]

8. Finding the nth-Term Formula

For an arithmetic sequence, identify the first term and common difference, then substitute them into:

\[ T_n=a+(n-1)d \]
Example:

Find the nth term of:

\(7,\ 12,\ 17,\ 22,\ldots\)
\[ a=7,\qquad d=5 \] Therefore: \[ T_n=7+(n-1)5 \] \[ T_n=7+5n-5 \] \[ \boxed{T_n=5n+2} \]

9. Finding a Particular Term

Example:

The nth term of a sequence is

\[ T_n=4n+3 \]

Find the 15th term.

\[ T_{15}=4(15)+3 \] \[ =60+3 \] \[ \boxed{63} \]

10. Finding the Position of a Term

Sometimes a term is given and we must find its position.

Example:

The nth term is

\[ T_n=3n+2 \]

Which term is 35?

\[ 3n+2=35 \] \[ 3n=33 \] \[ n=11 \] Therefore, 35 is the \[ \boxed{11^\text{th}\text{ term}} \]

11. Common Types of Simple Patterns

Adding a constant number

\(2,\ 5,\ 8,\ 11,\ldots\)

Rule: add 3.

Subtracting a constant number

\(40,\ 35,\ 30,\ 25,\ldots\)

Rule: subtract 5.

Multiplying by a constant number

\(2,\ 6,\ 18,\ 54,\ldots\)

Rule: multiply by 3.

Alternating patterns

\(2,\ 5,\ 4,\ 7,\ 6,\ 9,\ldots\)

Here, the pattern alternates between adding 3 and subtracting 1.

12. MANEB Examination Focus

In examination questions, you should be prepared to:

  • continue a sequence;
  • identify the pattern;
  • find missing terms;
  • find the common difference;
  • find the nth term;
  • find a specified term;
  • determine the position of a term;
  • use sequences in practical situations.
Exam strategy: Always write the difference between consecutive terms when dealing with an arithmetic sequence. This makes your method clear and reduces careless errors.

13. MANEB-Style Practice Questions

Question 1

Write the next three terms:

\(6,\ 10,\ 14,\ 18,\ldots\)
Question 2

Find the common difference of:

\(25,\ 21,\ 17,\ 13,\ldots\)
Question 3

Find the nth term of:

\(4,\ 9,\ 14,\ 19,\ldots\)
Question 4

Find the 30th term of:

\(2,\ 6,\ 10,\ 14,\ldots\)
Question 5

The nth term of a sequence is \(5n-2\). Find the 12th term.

Question 6

The nth term of a sequence is \(4n+1\). Which term is 49?

14. Answers

1. \(22,\ 26,\ 30\)

2. \(d=-4\)

3. \[ T_n=5n-1 \]

4. \[ T_{30}=118 \]

5. \[ T_{12}=58 \]

6. \[ 4n+1=49 \] \[ n=12 \] Therefore, 49 is the 12th term.

15. Common Mistakes

  • using the wrong common difference;
  • forgetting that \(n-1\) appears in the arithmetic sequence formula;
  • confusing the term number with the value of the term;
  • making arithmetic mistakes when expanding brackets;
  • assuming every sequence has a constant difference.

16. Examination Tips

  • Write at least the first few differences.
  • Check your formula using the first term.
  • Substitute the required value of \(n\) carefully.
  • Show your working, especially in examination questions.
  • Check whether your final answer actually appears in the sequence.
Remember: A sequence is about order and pattern. Do not only look at the numbers; look at how one term changes into the next.

17. Summary

  • A sequence is an ordered list of numbers.
  • The numbers are called terms.
  • An arithmetic sequence has a constant difference.
  • The common difference is represented by \(d\).
  • The nth term of an arithmetic sequence is \(T_n=a+(n-1)d\).
  • The nth-term formula can be used to find any term directly.
