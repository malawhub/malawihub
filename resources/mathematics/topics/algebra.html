1. Introduction to Algebra

Algebra is the branch of mathematics in which letters and symbols are used to represent numbers. It helps us describe unknown quantities and relationships between quantities.

Variable: A letter used to represent a number whose value may be unknown or may change.

Learning objectives

  • Identify terms, coefficients and constants.
  • Simplify algebraic expressions.
  • Expand and factorise expressions.
  • Solve linear equations.
  • Solve simultaneous equations.
  • Solve quadratic equations.
  • Apply algebra to practical and examination problems.

2. Algebraic Terms

Consider:

\[ 5x+3y-7 \]

The terms are \(5x\), \(3y\) and \(-7\).

Expression Coefficient Constant
\(3x+5\) 3 5
\(7y-4\) 7 -4
\(2x^2+3x-8\) 2 and 3 -8
Like terms: Terms containing the same variables raised to the same powers can be combined.
Simplify: \[ 3x+5x-2 \] Collect like terms: \[ 3x+5x=8x \] Therefore: \[ \boxed{8x-2} \]

3. Expansion of Brackets

Expansion removes brackets by multiplying each term inside the bracket.

\[ a(b+c)=ab+ac \]
Expand: \[ 3(x+4) \] Multiply 3 by both terms: \[ 3(x)+3(4) \] Therefore: \[ \boxed{3x+12} \]

Expansion of two brackets

\[ (a+b)(c+d)=ac+ad+bc+bd \]
Expand: \[ (x+3)(x+2) \] \[ =x^2+2x+3x+6 \] \[ =\boxed{x^2+5x+6} \]

4. Factorisation

Factorisation is the reverse process of expansion. It involves writing an expression as a product of factors.

Common factor

Factorise: \[ 6x+12 \] The highest common factor is 6. \[ 6x+12=\boxed{6(x+2)} \]

Difference of two squares

\[ a^2-b^2=(a-b)(a+b) \]
Factorise: \[ x^2-25 \] Since \(25=5^2\): \[ x^2-5^2=(x-5)(x+5) \] Therefore: \[ \boxed{(x-5)(x+5)} \]

Quadratic factorisation

Factorise: \[ x^2+7x+12 \] Find two numbers whose product is 12 and whose sum is 7: \[ 3\times4=12,\qquad3+4=7. \] Therefore: \[ \boxed{(x+3)(x+4)} \]

5. Linear Equations

A linear equation contains the unknown to the first power.

Solve: \[ 3x+5=20 \] Subtract 5 from both sides: \[ 3x=15 \] Divide by 3: \[ \boxed{x=5} \]
Rule: Whatever operation is performed on one side of an equation must also be performed on the other side.

6. Simultaneous Equations

Simultaneous equations are equations involving two or more unknowns that must be satisfied at the same time.

Solve: \[ x+y=9 \] \[ x-y=3 \] Add the equations: \[ 2x=12 \] Therefore: \[ x=6 \] Substitute into \(x+y=9\): \[ 6+y=9 \] \[ \boxed{y=3} \] Hence: \[ \boxed{x=6,\quad y=3} \]

7. Quadratic Equations

A quadratic equation has the form:

\[ ax^2+bx+c=0,\qquad a\ne0 \]

Solving by factorisation

Solve: \[ x^2-5x+6=0 \] Factorise: \[ (x-2)(x-3)=0 \] Therefore: \[ x-2=0\quad\text{or}\quad x-3=0 \] Hence: \[ \boxed{x=2\quad\text{or}\quad x=3} \]

Quadratic formula

\[ x=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a} \]

The quadratic formula is useful when a quadratic cannot easily be factorised.

8. Indices

Important laws of indices include:

\[ a^m\times a^n=a^{m+n} \]
\[ \frac{a^m}{a^n}=a^{m-n} \]
\[ (a^m)^n=a^{mn} \]
\[ a^0=1 \]
\[ a^{-n}=\frac{1}{a^n} \]
Simplify: \[ 2^3\times2^4 \] \[ =2^{3+4} \] \[ =2^7 \] \[ \boxed{128} \]

9. MANEB-style Practice Questions

  1. Simplify \(7x+3x-5\).
  2. Expand \((x+4)(x+2)\).
  3. Factorise \(x^2+7x+12\).
  4. Solve \(5x-7=18\).
  5. Solve \(x+y=12,\;x-y=4\).
  6. Solve \(x^2-9x+20=0\).
  7. Simplify \(3^2\times3^4\).
  8. Factorise \(9x^2-25\).

Answers

  1. \(10x-5\)
  2. \(x^2+6x+8\)
  3. \((x+3)(x+4)\)
  4. \(x=5\)
  5. \(x=8,\;y=4\)
  6. \(x=4,\;5\)
  7. \(729\)
  8. \((3x-5)(3x+5)\)
Examination tip: Always show your working. Pay particular attention to negative signs, brackets and the laws of indices. Check your answer by substituting it back into the original equation whenever possible.

10. Common Mistakes

  • Combining unlike terms, such as \(3x+4y=7xy\).
  • Forgetting to multiply every term when expanding brackets.
  • Changing a sign incorrectly when removing brackets.
  • Using the wrong quadratic formula values for \(a,b,c\).
  • Adding indices when dividing powers instead of subtracting them.
