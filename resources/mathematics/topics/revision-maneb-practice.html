1. How to Revise Mathematics

Effective mathematics revision requires understanding, practice and checking your mistakes. Do not only memorise formulas. Learn when and why each method is used.

  1. Review the basic concept.
  2. Write down important formulas.
  3. Study a worked example.
  4. Attempt a similar question without looking at the solution.
  5. Check your answer.
  6. Repeat questions you got wrong.

4. Mixed Practice

Question 1 — Number

Find the HCF of 36 and 60.

Question 2 — Algebra

Solve: \[ 3x+7=25 \]

Question 3 — Sequence

Find the 15th term of: \[ 4,\ 7,\ 10,\ 13,\ldots \]

Question 4 — Geometry

A rectangle has length 12 cm and width 7 cm. Find its area.

Question 5 — Trigonometry

In a right-angled triangle, the opposite side is 6 cm and the hypotenuse is 10 cm. Find \(\sin\theta\).

Question 6 — Statistics

Find the mean of: \[ 4,\ 6,\ 8,\ 10,\ 12 \]

Question 7 — Probability

A fair die is rolled once. Find the probability of obtaining an even number.

Question 8 — Financial Mathematics

An item costing MK 50,000 is sold for MK 60,000. Find the percentage profit.

5. Answers

1. \(12\)

2. \[ 3x+7=25 \] \[ 3x=18 \] \[ \boxed{x=6} \]

3. \[ T_{15}=4+(15-1)(3) \] \[ \boxed{46} \]

4. \[ 12\times7=\boxed{84\text{ cm}^2} \]

5. \[ \sin\theta=\frac{6}{10}=\boxed{0.6} \]

6. \[ \frac{4+6+8+10+12}{5}=\boxed{8} \]

7. There are 3 even outcomes: 2, 4 and 6. \[ P(E)=\frac36=\boxed{\frac12} \]

8. \[ \text{Profit}=60,000-50,000=10,000 \] \[ \frac{10,000}{50,000}\times100 =\boxed{20\%} \]

6. Examination Technique

  • Read every question carefully before calculating.
  • Underline important information.
  • Write formulas clearly.
  • Show your working.
  • Use correct mathematical units.
  • Check signs when working with negative numbers.
  • Check decimal places.
  • Use diagrams when they help explain the solution.
  • Do not spend too much time on one difficult question.
  • Return to unanswered questions before finishing.

7. Common Examination Mistakes

  • Copying numbers incorrectly from the question.
  • Using the wrong formula.
  • Skipping working and making an unexplained calculation.
  • Forgetting units.
  • Rounding too early.
  • Giving an answer that does not match the question.
  • Not checking whether the final answer is reasonable.

8. Final Revision Checklist

Before an examination, make sure you can:

  • work confidently with numbers and fractions;
  • simplify and solve algebraic expressions;
  • solve equations;
  • interpret graphs;
  • calculate areas, volumes and angles;
  • use trigonometric ratios;
  • calculate statistical measures;
  • solve probability problems;
  • work with vectors;
  • find terms in sequences;
  • solve financial mathematics problems.

9. Final Advice

Remember: Mathematics improves through practice. When you make a mistake, do not simply look at the correct answer. Find the step where your method went wrong and try the question again.

For examination preparation, practise questions from different topics rather than studying only one type of question. This helps you recognise which mathematical method is required.
