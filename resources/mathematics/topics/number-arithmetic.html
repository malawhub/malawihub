Number and Arithmetic

Mathematics • Secondary School

1. Introduction

Numbers are used to count, measure, compare quantities and describe relationships between quantities. Number and arithmetic form an important foundation for almost every other area of Mathematics.

In this topic we study different types of numbers and the basic operations involving numbers. We also learn about factors, multiples, fractions, decimals, percentages, ratios, proportion, approximation, standard form and directed numbers.

Important: A strong understanding of arithmetic makes algebra, geometry, statistics, probability and financial mathematics much easier.

2. Learning Objectives

By the end of this topic, a student should be able to:

  • Identify and classify different types of numbers.
  • Find factors and multiples of numbers.
  • Find the HCF and LCM of numbers.
  • Perform operations involving fractions.
  • Convert between fractions, decimals and percentages.
  • Solve problems involving ratio and proportion.
  • Round numbers to specified degrees of accuracy.
  • Use standard form correctly.
  • Perform calculations involving positive and negative numbers.
  • Apply order of operations correctly.
  • Solve numerical problems similar to examination questions.

3. Types of Numbers

3.1 Natural Numbers

Natural numbers are counting numbers.

\[ 1,\;2,\;3,\;4,\;5,\;6,\ldots \]

In some definitions, zero is also included. The convention used should therefore be understood from the question or textbook.

3.2 Whole Numbers

Whole numbers are the non-negative integers.

\[ 0,\;1,\;2,\;3,\;4,\ldots \]

3.3 Integers

Integers include positive numbers, negative numbers and zero.

\[ \ldots,-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3,\ldots \]

3.4 Even Numbers

An even number is an integer that is divisible by \(2\).

\[ 2,\;4,\;6,\;8,\;10,\;12,\ldots \]

3.5 Odd Numbers

An odd number is an integer that is not divisible by \(2\).

\[ 1,\;3,\;5,\;7,\;9,\;11,\ldots \]

3.6 Prime Numbers

A prime number is a whole number greater than \(1\) that has exactly two factors: \(1\) and itself.

Examples include:

\[ 2,\;3,\;5,\;7,\;11,\;13,\;17,\;19,\ldots \]

The number \(1\) is not a prime number.

3.7 Composite Numbers

A composite number has more than two factors.

\[ 4,\;6,\;8,\;9,\;10,\;12,\ldots \]

4. Factors and Multiples

4.1 Factors

A factor of a number divides that number exactly, leaving no remainder.

For example, the factors of \(12\) are:

\[ 1,\;2,\;3,\;4,\;6,\;12 \]

4.2 Multiples

Multiples are obtained by multiplying a number by whole numbers.

The first five positive multiples of \(6\) are:

\[ 6,\;12,\;18,\;24,\;30 \]

4.3 Prime Factorisation

Prime factorisation means expressing a number as a product of prime numbers.

Example 1: Find the prime factorisation of \(60\).
\[ 60=2\times30 \] \[ =2\times2\times15 \] \[ =2\times2\times3\times5 \] Therefore, \[ \boxed{60=2^2\times3\times5} \]

4.4 Highest Common Factor (HCF)

The HCF of two or more numbers is the largest factor common to all the numbers.

Example 2: Find the HCF of \(18\) and \(24\).
Factors of \(18\): \[ 1,\;2,\;3,\;6,\;9,\;18 \] Factors of \(24\): \[ 1,\;2,\;3,\;4,\;6,\;8,\;12,\;24 \] Common factors: \[ 1,\;2,\;3,\;6 \] Therefore, \[ \boxed{\operatorname{HCF}=6} \]

4.5 Lowest Common Multiple (LCM)

The LCM is the smallest positive number that is a multiple of two or more numbers.

Example 3: Find the LCM of \(6\) and \(8\).
Multiples of \(6\): \[ 6,\;12,\;18,\;24,\;30,\ldots \] Multiples of \(8\): \[ 8,\;16,\;24,\;32,\ldots \] The first common multiple is \(24\). \[ \boxed{\operatorname{LCM}=24} \]

5. Fractions

A fraction represents a part of a whole or a division of one number by another.
\[ \frac{a}{b},\qquad b\neq0 \]

The number above the line is called the numerator. The number below the line is called the denominator.

\[ \frac{\text{numerator}}{\text{denominator}} \]

5.1 Proper Fractions

A proper fraction has a numerator smaller than its denominator.

\[ \frac{2}{5},\qquad \frac{3}{7},\qquad \frac{5}{8} \]

5.2 Improper Fractions

An improper fraction has a numerator greater than or equal to its denominator.

\[ \frac{7}{4},\qquad \frac{9}{5},\qquad \frac{12}{6} \]

5.3 Mixed Numbers

A mixed number contains a whole number and a proper fraction.

\[ 2\frac{1}{3},\qquad 4\frac{2}{5} \]

5.4 Equivalent Fractions

Equivalent fractions have the same value even though their numerators and denominators may be different.

\[ \frac{1}{2} = \frac{2}{4} = \frac{3}{6} = \frac{4}{8} \]

5.5 Simplifying Fractions

To simplify a fraction, divide the numerator and denominator by their highest common factor.

Example 4: Simplify \(\frac{18}{24}\).
The HCF of \(18\) and \(24\) is \(6\). \[ \frac{18}{24} = \frac{18\div6}{24\div6} \] \[ = \boxed{\frac{3}{4}} \]

5.6 Adding Fractions with the Same Denominator

When fractions have the same denominator, add the numerators and keep the denominator unchanged.

Example 5: Calculate
\[ \frac{2}{7}+\frac{3}{7} \]
\[ \frac{2}{7}+\frac{3}{7} = \frac{2+3}{7} \] \[ = \boxed{\frac{5}{7}} \]

5.7 Adding Fractions with Different Denominators

First find a common denominator, usually by finding the LCM of the denominators.

Example 6: Calculate
\[ \frac{2}{3}+\frac{1}{6} \]
The LCM of \(3\) and \(6\) is \(6\). \[ \frac{2}{3} = \frac{4}{6} \] Therefore, \[ \frac{2}{3}+\frac{1}{6} = \frac{4}{6}+\frac{1}{6} \] \[ = \boxed{\frac{5}{6}} \]

5.8 Subtracting Fractions

Example 7: Calculate
\[ \frac{5}{6}-\frac{1}{4} \]
The LCM of \(6\) and \(4\) is \(12\). \[ \frac{5}{6} = \frac{10}{12} \] and \[ \frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{12} \] Therefore, \[ \frac{5}{6}-\frac{1}{4} = \frac{10}{12}-\frac{3}{12} \] \[ = \boxed{\frac{7}{12}} \]

5.9 Multiplying Fractions

Multiply the numerators together and the denominators together.

Example 8: Calculate
\[ \frac{2}{3}\times\frac{5}{7} \]
\[ \frac{2\times5}{3\times7} = \frac{10}{21} \] Therefore, \[ \boxed{\frac{10}{21}} \]

5.10 Dividing Fractions

To divide by a fraction, multiply by its reciprocal.

\[ \frac{a}{b}\div\frac{c}{d} = \frac{a}{b}\times\frac{d}{c} \]
Example 9: Calculate
\[ \frac{3}{4}\div\frac{2}{5} \]
Change division to multiplication and invert the second fraction. \[ \frac{3}{4}\times\frac{5}{2} \] \[ = \frac{15}{8} \] Therefore, \[ \boxed{\frac{15}{8}} \] As a mixed number: \[ \boxed{1\frac{7}{8}} \]

5.11 Converting a Mixed Number to an Improper Fraction

Example 10: Convert \(2\frac{3}{5}\) to an improper fraction.
Multiply the whole number by the denominator: \[ 2\times5=10 \] Add the numerator: \[ 10+3=13 \] Keep the denominator: \[ \boxed{2\frac{3}{5}=\frac{13}{5}} \]

6. Decimals

A decimal is another way of writing a number using a decimal point. The digits to the right of the decimal point represent parts of a whole.

Key idea: In a decimal number, the position of each digit determines its value.

6.1 Place Value in Decimals

Consider the number \(37.482\). The digits have the following place values:

Tens Units Decimal Point Tenths Hundredths Thousandths
3 7 . 4 8 2

Therefore,

\[ 37.482 = 30+7+\frac{4}{10}+\frac{8}{100}+\frac{2}{1000} \]

6.2 Reading and Writing Decimals

Decimals can be read by stating the whole-number part followed by the decimal part according to its place value.

Example 1

Write \(5.27\) in words.

\[ 5.27 = 5+\frac{27}{100} \]

Therefore, \(5.27\) can be read as five and twenty-seven hundredths.

6.3 Converting Fractions to Decimals

A fraction can be converted to a decimal by dividing the numerator by the denominator.

Example 2

Convert \(\frac{3}{4}\) to a decimal.

\[ \frac{3}{4}=3\div4=0.75 \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{\frac{3}{4}=0.75} \]

Example 3

Convert \(\frac{7}{8}\) to a decimal.

\[ \frac{7}{8}=7\div8=0.875 \] \[ \boxed{\frac{7}{8}=0.875} \]

6.4 Converting Decimals to Fractions

To convert a terminating decimal to a fraction, write the decimal over \(10\), \(100\), \(1000\), and so on, depending on the number of decimal places. Then simplify the fraction.

Example 4

Convert \(0.45\) to a fraction in its simplest form.

\[ 0.45=\frac{45}{100} \] \[ \frac{45}{100}=\frac{9}{20} \] \[ \boxed{0.45=\frac{9}{20}} \]

6.5 Comparing and Ordering Decimals

To compare decimals, compare the digits from left to right. If necessary, add zeros to the end of a decimal without changing its value.

Example 5

Arrange \(0.7,\;0.65,\;0.705,\;0.68\) in ascending order.

Write them with the same number of decimal places:

\[ 0.700,\quad0.650,\quad0.705,\quad0.680 \]

Therefore,

\[ \boxed{0.650<0.680<0.700<0.705} \]

Hence the required order is:

\[ \boxed{0.65,\;0.68,\;0.7,\;0.705} \]

6.6 Addition and Subtraction of Decimals

When adding or subtracting decimals, line up the decimal points. Zeros may be added where necessary.

Example 6

Calculate \(12.75+3.608\).

\[ \begin{array}{r} 12.750\\ +\;3.608\\ \hline 16.358 \end{array} \] \[ \boxed{12.75+3.608=16.358} \]

Example 7

Calculate \(15.2-7.845\).

\[ \begin{array}{r} 15.200\\ -\;7.845\\ \hline 7.355 \end{array} \] \[ \boxed{15.2-7.845=7.355} \]

6.7 Multiplication of Decimals

Multiply the numbers as whole numbers first. Then place the decimal point in the answer according to the total number of decimal places in the factors.

Example 8

Calculate \(2.4\times1.35\).

\[ 24\times135=3240 \]

There are three decimal places altogether, so:

\[ 2.4\times1.35=3.240=3.24 \] \[ \boxed{2.4\times1.35=3.24} \]

6.8 Division of Decimals

When dividing by a decimal, the divisor can be changed into a whole number by moving its decimal point. The decimal point in the dividend must be moved the same number of places.

Example 9

Calculate \(7.2\div0.6\).

\[ 7.2\div0.6=72\div6 \] \[ 72\div6=12 \] \[ \boxed{7.2\div0.6=12} \]

6.9 Terminating and Recurring Decimals

A terminating decimal ends after a finite number of decimal places.

Examples include:

\[ 0.5,\qquad0.25,\qquad0.125,\qquad2.75 \]

A recurring decimal contains a digit or group of digits that repeats indefinitely.

\[ 0.333\ldots=0.\overline{3} \] \[ 0.272727\ldots=0.\overline{27} \]

6.10 Rounding Decimals

To round a decimal, identify the required place value and look at the digit immediately to its right.

Rounding rule: If the next digit is \(5\) or greater, increase the required digit by \(1\). If the next digit is less than \(5\), leave the required digit unchanged.

Example 10

Round \(7.846\) to two decimal places.

The second decimal place is \(4\). The next digit is \(6\), so increase \(4\) to \(5\).

\[ \boxed{7.846\approx7.85} \]

6.11 Decimal Applications

Decimals are commonly used in money, measurements, distances, masses, temperatures, examination marks and other practical calculations.

Example 11

A student buys a book for MK 2,750.50 and a pen for MK 450.75. How much does the student spend altogether?

\[ \begin{array}{r} 2750.50\\ +450.75\\ \hline 3201.25 \end{array} \] \[ \boxed{\text{Total}=MK\,3,201.25} \]

6.12 MANEB Examination Focus

Questions involving decimals may test your ability to:

  • identify decimal place values;
  • convert fractions to decimals and decimals to fractions;
  • compare and order decimal numbers;
  • perform the four basic operations with decimals;
  • round numbers to a specified number of decimal places;
  • solve practical problems involving money and measurements.

MANEB-style Question 1

Calculate \[ 14.75-3.608+2.45. \]

\[ 14.750-3.608=11.142 \] \[ 11.142+2.450=13.592 \] \[ \boxed{13.592} \]

MANEB-style Question 2

A trader has \(25.5\) kg of maize and sells \(7.75\) kg. How many kilograms remain?

\[ 25.50-7.75=17.75 \] \[ \boxed{17.75\text{ kg}} \]

6.15 Common Mistakes

  • Failing to line up decimal points when adding or subtracting.
  • Counting decimal places incorrectly during multiplication.
  • Moving the decimal point in only one number during division.
  • Rounding before completing a calculation when exact working is required.
  • Forgetting to simplify a fraction after converting a decimal.
Examination tip: Always show your working. In calculations involving several decimal places, keep the decimal points aligned and check whether your final answer is reasonable.
