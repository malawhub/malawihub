1. Introduction

Trigonometry is the study of relationships between the sides and angles of triangles. It is especially useful for finding unknown lengths and angles.

Learning objectives

  • Identify the sides of a right-angled triangle.
  • Use sine, cosine and tangent.
  • Find unknown sides.
  • Find unknown angles.
  • Use Pythagoras' theorem with trigonometry.
  • Solve practical problems involving heights and distances.
  • Apply trigonometry to examination questions.

2. Right-Angled Triangles

A right-angled triangle contains one angle equal to \(90^\circ\).

The three important sides

  • Hypotenuse: the side opposite the \(90^\circ\) angle.
  • Opposite: the side opposite the angle being considered.
  • Adjacent: the side next to the angle being considered, excluding the hypotenuse.

3. SOH CAH TOA

The three basic trigonometric ratios are remembered using:

\[ \boxed{\text{SOH}} \qquad \boxed{\text{CAH}} \qquad \boxed{\text{TOA}} \]
Ratio Formula
Sine \(\sin\theta=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}\)
Cosine \(\cos\theta=\frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}\)
Tangent \(\tan\theta=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}\)
SOH: Sine = Opposite ÷ Hypotenuse
CAH: Cosine = Adjacent ÷ Hypotenuse
TOA: Tangent = Opposite ÷ Adjacent

4. Finding an Unknown Side

A right-angled triangle has an angle of \(30^\circ\) and a hypotenuse of \(10\) cm. Find the opposite side. Use sine: \[ \sin30^\circ=\frac{\text{opposite}}{10} \] Therefore: \[ \text{opposite}=10\sin30^\circ \] Since: \[ \sin30^\circ=\frac12 \] \[ \boxed{\text{opposite}=5\text{ cm}} \]

5. Finding an Unknown Angle

When the angle is unknown, use the inverse trigonometric functions:

\[ \sin^{-1},\qquad \cos^{-1},\qquad \tan^{-1} \]
A right-angled triangle has opposite side \(6\) cm and adjacent side \(8\) cm. Find angle \(\theta\). \[ \tan\theta=\frac68 \] \[ \tan\theta=0.75 \] Therefore: \[ \theta=\tan^{-1}(0.75) \] \[ \boxed{\theta\approx36.9^\circ} \]

6. Pythagoras and Trigonometry

Pythagoras' theorem states:

\[ a^2+b^2=c^2 \]

where \(c\) is the hypotenuse.

A right-angled triangle has shorter sides \(6\) cm and \(8\) cm. \[ c^2=6^2+8^2 \] \[ c^2=36+64=100 \] \[ \boxed{c=10\text{ cm}} \]

8. Heights and Distances

Trigonometry can be used to calculate heights and distances that cannot be measured directly.

A person stands \(20\) m from the base of a vertical tree. The angle of elevation to the top is \(40^\circ\). Find the height of the tree. \[ \tan40^\circ=\frac{h}{20} \] Therefore: \[ h=20\tan40^\circ \] \[ \boxed{h\approx16.8\text{ m}} \]
An angle of elevation is measured upwards from the horizontal. An angle of depression is measured downwards from the horizontal.

9. Bearings and Trigonometry

Bearings are measured clockwise from North and are normally written as three-figure angles.

Examples:

  • East = \(090^\circ\)
  • South = \(180^\circ\)
  • West = \(270^\circ\)
  • North = \(000^\circ\)
Always draw a diagram when solving a bearing problem.

10. MANEB-style Practice

  1. A right-angled triangle has opposite side \(9\) cm and hypotenuse \(15\) cm. Find the angle.
  2. A ladder \(5\) m long rests against a vertical wall. The ladder makes an angle of \(60^\circ\) with the ground. Find the height reached by the ladder.
  3. Find the missing side if the adjacent side is \(8\) cm and the angle is \(35^\circ\).
  4. A tree is \(15\) m from an observer. The angle of elevation to its top is \(50^\circ\). Find its height.
  5. A right-angled triangle has sides \(7\) cm and \(24\) cm. Find the hypotenuse.
  6. Find \(\theta\) if \(\cos\theta=0.6\), where \(0^\circ<\theta<90^\circ\).

Answers

  1. \(\theta\approx36.9^\circ\)
  2. \(5\sin60^\circ\approx4.33\) m
  3. \(8\tan35^\circ\approx5.60\) cm
  4. \(15\tan50^\circ\approx17.88\) m
  5. \(25\) cm
  6. \(\theta\approx53.1^\circ\)

11. Common Mistakes

  • Choosing the wrong trigonometric ratio.
  • Confusing opposite and adjacent sides.
  • Using the hypotenuse as the adjacent side.
  • Forgetting to use degree mode on the calculator.
  • Using \(\tan^{-1}\) when the question requires sine or cosine.
  • Rounding too early.
  • Failing to draw a diagram for word problems.
Examination tip: First mark the given angle, identify the opposite, adjacent and hypotenuse sides, then choose SOH, CAH or TOA. Keep your calculator in degree mode unless the question specifically states otherwise.
