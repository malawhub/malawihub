1. Introduction

Financial mathematics applies mathematics to money and everyday financial situations. It includes profit and loss, discounts, interest, commission, wages, taxation and exchange rates.

2. Learning Objectives

  • Calculate profit and loss.
  • Calculate percentage profit and loss.
  • Calculate discounts.
  • Calculate simple and compound interest.
  • Solve commission and wage problems.
  • Work with deductions and net pay.
  • Solve exchange-rate problems.
  • Apply financial mathematics in examination questions.

3. Important Terms

TermMeaning
Cost priceAmount paid to obtain an item.
Selling priceAmount for which an item is sold.
ProfitGain when selling price is greater than cost price.
LossAmount lost when selling price is less than cost price.
DiscountReduction from the marked price.
PrincipalOriginal amount invested or borrowed.
InterestMoney earned or charged for using money.
CommissionPayment based on a percentage of sales.

4. Profit and Loss

Profit

\[\text{Profit}=\text{Selling Price}-\text{Cost Price}\]

Loss

\[\text{Loss}=\text{Cost Price}-\text{Selling Price}\]
Example:

An item costs MK 25,000 and is sold for MK 30,000. Find the profit.

\[ 30,000-25,000=5,000 \] \[ \boxed{\text{Profit}=MK\,5,000} \]
Remember: Percentage profit and loss are normally calculated using the cost price as the base.
\[ \text{Percentage Profit}= \frac{\text{Profit}}{\text{Cost Price}}\times100\% \]
\[ \text{Percentage Loss}= \frac{\text{Loss}}{\text{Cost Price}}\times100\% \]
Example:

An item costs MK 40,000 and sells for MK 50,000. Find the percentage profit.

\[ \text{Profit}=50,000-40,000=10,000 \] \[ \frac{10,000}{40,000}\times100=25\% \] \[ \boxed{25\%} \]

5. Discount

A discount is a reduction from the marked price.

\[ \text{Discount}= \text{Marked Price}-\text{Selling Price} \]
\[ \text{Percentage Discount}= \frac{\text{Discount}}{\text{Marked Price}}\times100\% \]
Example:

A television marked at MK 500,000 is discounted by 12%. Find the selling price.

\[ \text{Discount}=\frac{12}{100}\times500,000=60,000 \] \[ \text{Selling Price}=500,000-60,000 \] \[ \boxed{MK\,440,000} \]

6. Simple Interest

\[ I=\frac{PRT}{100} \] where \(P\) is principal, \(R\) is rate and \(T\) is time in years.
\[ A=P+I \]
Example:

Find the simple interest on MK 200,000 for 3 years at 8% per year.

\[ I=\frac{200,000\times8\times3}{100} \] \[ \boxed{I=MK\,48,000} \] \[ A=200,000+48,000 \] \[ \boxed{A=MK\,248,000} \]

7. Compound Interest

Compound interest is calculated on the principal plus interest already accumulated.

\[ A=P\left(1+\frac{r}{100}\right)^n \]

where \(A\) is the final amount, \(P\) is the principal, \(r\) is the rate and \(n\) is the number of periods.

Example:

Find the amount when MK 100,000 is invested for 2 years at 10% compound interest per year.

\[ A=100,000(1.10)^2 \] \[ A=100,000(1.21) \] \[ \boxed{A=MK\,121,000} \]

8. Commission

\[ \text{Commission}= \frac{\text{Rate}}{100}\times\text{Sales} \]
Example:

A salesperson receives 5% commission on sales of MK 800,000. Find the commission.

\[ \frac{5}{100}\times800,000 = \boxed{MK\,40,000} \]

9. Wages and Overtime

Wages may be calculated using hourly rates, daily rates or fixed salaries.

Example:

A worker earns MK 1,500 per hour and works for 40 hours. Find the basic wage.

\[ 1,500\times40 = \boxed{MK\,60,000} \]

Overtime

Overtime is work done beyond normal working hours and may be paid at a higher rate.

Example:

A worker earns MK 2,000 per hour. Overtime is paid at 1.5 times the normal rate. Find the pay for 4 overtime hours.

\[ 1.5\times2,000=3,000 \] \[ 3,000\times4=\boxed{MK\,12,000} \]

10. Gross Pay, Deductions and Net Pay

\[ \text{Net Pay}= \text{Gross Pay}-\text{Total Deductions} \]
Example:

A worker earns MK 250,000 and has deductions of MK 35,000. Find the net pay.

\[ 250,000-35,000 = \boxed{MK\,215,000} \]

11. Exchange Rates

An exchange rate tells us how much one currency is worth in another currency.

Example:

Suppose MK 2,000 is equivalent to 1 unit of another currency. How much is MK 100,000 in that currency?

\[ \frac{100,000}{2,000}=50 \] \[ \boxed{50\text{ units}} \]
Important: Real exchange rates change. Always use the current rate supplied by the relevant financial service.

12. Financial Problem-Solving Method

  1. Read the question carefully.
  2. Write down the information given.
  3. Identify what must be found.
  4. Choose the correct formula.
  5. Substitute the values.
  6. Calculate carefully.
  7. Include the correct unit or currency.
  8. Check whether the answer is reasonable.

14. MANEB-Style Practice Questions

Question 1

A trader buys an item for MK 80,000 and sells it for MK 96,000.

Find:

  1. the profit;
  2. the percentage profit.
Question 2

A shirt marked at MK 25,000 is given a 20% discount. Find the selling price.

Question 3

Calculate the simple interest on MK 150,000 at 6% per year for 4 years.

Question 4

Find the amount obtained when MK 200,000 is invested at 5% compound interest per year for 2 years.

Question 5

A salesperson receives 4% commission on sales of MK 1,250,000. Calculate the commission.

Question 6

A worker earns MK 180,000 and has deductions of MK 27,500. Find the net pay.

15. Answers

1(a). MK 16,000

1(b). \(20\%\)

2. MK 20,000

3. MK 36,000

4. MK 220,500

5. MK 50,000

6. MK 152,500

16. Common Mistakes

  • Using selling price instead of cost price for percentage profit.
  • Forgetting to convert a percentage correctly.
  • Confusing simple interest with compound interest.
  • Forgetting to subtract deductions from gross pay.
  • Using an exchange rate in the wrong direction.
  • Leaving out the currency or percentage unit.

17. Examination Tips

  • Write the formula before substituting values.
  • Keep money calculations organised.
  • Check whether the question asks for interest or total amount.
  • Identify the correct base quantity when calculating percentages.
  • Show all important working.
  • Check that the final answer is reasonable.
Remember: Financial mathematics is not only about calculating numbers. Understand what each number represents before choosing a formula.

18. Summary

  • Profit = Selling Price − Cost Price.
  • Loss = Cost Price − Selling Price.
  • Percentage profit and loss use cost price as the base.
  • Discount reduces the marked price.
  • Simple interest is calculated on the original principal.
  • Compound interest includes accumulated interest.
  • Commission is usually a percentage of sales.
  • Net pay = Gross Pay − Total Deductions.
  • Exchange rates are used to convert currencies.
