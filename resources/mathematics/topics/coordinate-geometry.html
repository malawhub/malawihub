1. Introduction

Coordinate geometry combines algebra and geometry. It uses coordinates, equations and graphs to describe the position of points and the properties of lines.

Learning objectives

  • Plot and interpret coordinates.
  • Calculate gradients.
  • Find equations of straight lines.
  • Identify parallel and perpendicular lines.
  • Calculate distances between points.
  • Find midpoints.
  • Apply coordinate geometry to examination problems.

2. Cartesian Coordinates

A point is written as:

\[ (x,y) \]

The \(x\)-coordinate gives the horizontal position and the \(y\)-coordinate gives the vertical position.

The origin is \((0,0)\).

3. Gradient of a Line

The gradient describes the steepness of a straight line.

\[ m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1} \]
Find the gradient of the line passing through \(A(2,3)\) and \(B(6,11)\). \[ m=\frac{11-3}{6-2} \] \[ =\frac84 \] \[ \boxed{m=2} \]
A horizontal line has gradient \(0\). A vertical line has an undefined gradient.

4. Equation of a Straight Line

The general form is:

\[ y=mx+c \]

where:

  • \(m\) is the gradient;
  • \(c\) is the \(y\)-intercept.
Find the equation of a line with gradient \(3\) passing through \((2,5)\). Use: \[ y-y_1=m(x-x_1) \] Therefore: \[ y-5=3(x-2) \] \[ y-5=3x-6 \] \[ \boxed{y=3x-1} \]

5. Parallel Lines

Parallel lines never meet and have equal gradients.

\[ m_1=m_2 \]
The line \[ y=4x+2 \] has gradient 4. Any line parallel to it must also have gradient: \[ \boxed{4} \]

6. Perpendicular Lines

Two non-vertical lines are perpendicular when the product of their gradients is \(-1\).

\[ m_1m_2=-1 \]
If one line has gradient \(4\), the perpendicular gradient is: \[ 4m=-1 \] \[ \boxed{m=-\frac14} \]

7. Distance Between Two Points

For points \((x_1,y_1)\) and \((x_2,y_2)\):

\[ d=\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2} \]
Find the distance between \((1,2)\) and \((4,6)\). \[ d=\sqrt{(4-1)^2+(6-2)^2} \] \[ =\sqrt{3^2+4^2} \] \[ =\sqrt{25} \] \[ \boxed{d=5} \]

8. Midpoint of a Line Segment

\[ M= \left( \frac{x_1+x_2}{2}, \frac{y_1+y_2}{2} \right) \]
Find the midpoint of \(A(2,4)\) and \(B(8,10)\). \[ M= \left( \frac{2+8}{2}, \frac{4+10}{2} \right) \] \[ M=(5,7) \] Therefore: \[ \boxed{(5,7)} \]

9. Finding an Unknown Coordinate

The gradient formula can be rearranged to find an unknown coordinate.

The gradient between \(A(2,3)\) and \(B(x,9)\) is 2. Find \(x\). \[ 2=\frac{9-3}{x-2} \] \[ 2=\frac6{x-2} \] \[ 2(x-2)=6 \] \[ 2x-4=6 \] \[ 2x=10 \] \[ \boxed{x=5} \]

10. Coordinate Geometry and Triangles

Coordinates can be used to determine whether a triangle is right-angled, isosceles or has other special properties.

For example, distances between the three vertices can be calculated and compared.

When a question asks you to prove that a triangle is right-angled, Pythagoras' theorem can be used after finding the side lengths.

11. MANEB-style Practice

  1. Find the gradient through \((1,2)\) and \((5,10)\).
  2. Find the equation of a line with gradient \(2\) passing through \((1,4)\).
  3. Find the distance between \((0,0)\) and \((6,8)\).
  4. Find the midpoint of \((2,5)\) and \((8,11)\).
  5. State the gradient of a line perpendicular to a line of gradient 5.
  6. The gradient between \((3,4)\) and \((x,10)\) is 2. Find \(x\).
  7. Are the lines \(y=3x+1\) and \(y=3x-7\) parallel? Give a reason.

Answers

  1. \(2\)
  2. \(y=2x+2\)
  3. \(10\)
  4. \((5,8)\)
  5. \(-\frac15\)
  6. \(x=6\)
  7. Yes. They have equal gradients of 3.

12. Common Mistakes

  • Reversing the order of coordinates.
  • Using \(x\)-values where \(y\)-values should be used.
  • Forgetting brackets when substituting negative coordinates.
  • Confusing the gradient with the \(y\)-intercept.
  • Using the wrong sign for perpendicular gradients.
  • Forgetting to square both differences in the distance formula.
Examination tip: Write down the relevant formula before substituting values. This reduces errors and makes your method clear to the examiner.
